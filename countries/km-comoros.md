## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ***Udzima wa ya Masiwa*** (Comorian)
- **Title (English):** "The Union of the Great Islands"
- **Adopted:** 1978
- **Words by:** Saïd Hachim Sidi Abdérémane
- **Music by:** Saïd Hachim Sidi Abdérémane and Kamildine Abdallah
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> 🔴 **The anthem names an island the country does not govern.** Source [2]: it "references all four
> islands by name: **Maori, Ndzuwani, Mwali and Ngazidja**" — but only three are internationally
> recognised as Comoros. **Maori is Mayotte**, which voted against independence in 1974 and is
> administered by France as an overseas department [1].
>
> **This is a territorial claim sung as a national anthem.** Source [1] records that France has
> "repeatedly vetoed United Nations Security Council resolutions" affirming Comorian sovereignty over
> Mayotte, and that Mayotte became a French overseas department and region in 2011.
>
> ⚠️ **Handle exactly as [`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md) handles Western Sahara.** The app should say
> that sovereignty is disputed and name whose claims are in contest — the Comoros' and France's — and
> stop there. **An anthem is not a neutral object when its lyrics are a claim**, and playing it
> without context would have the app assert a position on a live UN dispute.
> The title in the app's own list should stay as it is.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **"The 18th coup since 1975"** [1] — eighteen in twenty-four years. State it with the source, and
  resist the framing that follows easily from it: **a country of under a million people, whose
  territory was divided at independence by the departing power, is not simply "unstable".** The
  Mayotte partition and the coup record are connected `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, and the causal claim needs
  a source before it is made.
- **933's "Perfume Islands"** [1] — the islands were known to Indian Ocean traders a thousand years
  ago. **This folder's Eastern African files are beginning to describe a maritime world** — Omani
  sailors, the Swahili coast, monsoon trade — **entirely distinct from the Atlantic story the West
  African files tell.** Worth naming as such.
- **The islands unified in 1908 and divided in 1974–75** [1]. A single administration for 67 years,
  then a referendum that split it.

## Peoples & cultures

**2000 data** [1]: **Comorians 97.1% · Makua 1.6% · others 1.3%.** Population estimated at **944,388**
(2026) [1].

- **97.1% one group** [1] — with Equatorial Guinea's Fang at 85.7%
  ([`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md)), among the most homogeneous in the folder.
- ⚠️ **The Makua (1.6%)** are also a major people of **Mozambique**
  ([`mz-mozambique.md`](mz-mozambique.md)) — their presence here is likely a trace of the Indian Ocean
  slave trade `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Do not assert the connection without a source, but do not miss it
  either**: it would tie this small population directly to the eastern counterpart of the Atlantic
  trade documented across the West African files.
- **Islam is central to the country's identity** — the 1978 state was the "Federal **Islamic**
  Republic" [1] and the 2026 heritage inscription is of **Islamic sites** [1]. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for
  anything beyond that.

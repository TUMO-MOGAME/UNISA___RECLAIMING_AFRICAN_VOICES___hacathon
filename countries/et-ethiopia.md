**Researched by:** Tumo Olorato Mogame, with Claude · **Last updated:** 2026-08-30
**Status:** core research — languages, script, anthem, milestones, peoples and heritage sourced · literature partial

> **How this file is put together.** Built **claim by claim**: every row names the source it came
> from, and the numbered list at the bottom is what those names point at. Sources are
> encyclopaedia-grade — rough sources are fine at this stage ([README.md](README.md)), assertions made
> to a reader are not.
>
> 🔴 **Ethiopia is the most important country in this folder for Ubuntu Heritage's central claim.**
> It has **five official African languages**, an **indigenous script in continuous use for roughly
> two thousand years**, a **written literature in its own language**, and it was **never fully
> colonised**. Every assumption the phrase "reclaiming African voices" implicitly makes about loss is
> complicated here — and productively so.

*national* rather than official; **Tigrinya is shared with Eritrea** ([`er-eritrea.md`](er-eritrea.md)).

## 🔴 The Ge'ez script — an African writing system still in daily use

| | |
|---|---|
| **What** | **Ge'ez / Ethiopic**, an indigenous African script [1] |
| **The language it began with** | Ge'ez — "the ancient Semitic language of Ethiopia" [1] |
| **Still used for** | **Amharic and Tigrinya**, both official languages today [1] |
| **Oldest attestations here** | The **Monumentum Adulitanum**, a **3rd-century Aksumite inscription** [1] |
| **A named classical text** | The **Book of Axum**, a **15th-century Ge'ez text** with legendary accounts of Ethiopia's founding [1] |

🔴 **This is the third indigenous African script this research has found — and by far the oldest and
most alive.**

| Script | Where | When | Status |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ge'ez / Ethiopic** | Ethiopia, Eritrea | attested by the **3rd century AD** | **In everyday official use today** |
| **Vai** | Liberia ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)) | c. **1833** | In use |
| **N'Ko** | Guinea ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)) | **1949** | In use |

**Liberia's file records the claim that "at least 27 new scripts have been invented for West African
languages" since the 1830s** ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)). **Ge'ez shows the practice is not a
nineteenth-century novelty — Africans have been writing African languages in African scripts for
approximately two thousand years.**

**For the submission narrative, this is the strongest single argument available.** The premise that
African heritage is *oral* and therefore fragile is true of a great deal — and demonstrably false as
a general claim. Ubuntu Heritage should hold both: **Hampâté Bâ's burning library**
([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)) *and* Ge'ez, Timbuktu's million manuscripts
([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)), Vai and N'Ko.

⚠️ **Sourcing caveat:** the Ge'ez facts above come from source [1]'s passing references. **This
deserves a proper scholarly source before it carries as much weight as it should.** Top open question.

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ወደፊት ገስግሺ ውድ እናት ኢትዮጵያ (Amharic)
- **Title (English):** "March Forward, Dear Mother Ethiopia"
- **Adopted:** **1992**
- **Words by:** Dereje Melaku Mengesha
- **Music by:** Solomon Lulu
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The title is written in Ge'ez script** — the only anthem in this folder whose own-language title
> uses an indigenous African writing system. Every other file records a title in Latin or Arabic
> script.
>
> **Adopted in 1992, after the communist government fell** [2]. Source [2] quotes Ethiopia's
> Constitution: the anthem should "reflect the ideals of the Constitution, the Commitment of the
> Peoples of Ethiopia to live together in a democratic order and of their common destiny" — **a
> constitutional specification of what a national anthem is for**, which no other country in this
> research provides.
>
> **The tenth politically-changed anthem in this folder.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **Adwa, 1896.** An African army defeated a European colonial invasion in open battle, and the
  independence held. **This is the fact that most needs to be in Ubuntu Heritage**, and it should be
  told beside Dimawe (Botswana, 1852, [`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)), Samori Touré's twenty-year
  resistance ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)) and Omar Mukhtar ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)). **The
  folder now documents African military resistance to colonisation across five countries — and in
  Ethiopia's case, it won.**
- ⚠️ **"Never fully colonised" needs its qualifier kept.** Source [1] says Ethiopia and **Liberia**
  were "the only African country to never be fully colonised" — and it also records a **five-year
  Italian occupation, 1936–1941** [1]. Both are true; the app must say both. **And note the pairing:
  Ethiopia and Liberia ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)) — one that repelled empire, one founded by
  the formerly enslaved. Two very different routes to the same category.**
- **This is the oldest human record on Earth** [3]. Lucy, Melka Kunture's 1.7-million-year deposits
  and the Omo tools are in three separate World Heritage inscriptions. **The app's Journey narrative
  about the peopling of Africa starts here**, before it reaches Gabon's Bantu migration
  ([`ga-gabon.md`](ga-gabon.md)) or Southern Africa.
- ⚠️ **The Derg period (1974–91) was extremely violent** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — source [1] records only
  the dates. As with Equatorial Guinea's Macías ([`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md)),
  **a neutral date-list here would be a false account.** Source properly or leave out.
- ⚠️ **Ethiopia's recent conflicts, including in Tigray, are absent from source [1]'s milestones.**
  Do not publish a timeline that stops at 1991.

| Others | 12.6% |

Over **80 ethnic groups** in total [1].

- ⚠️ **The census is from 2007** — nearly twenty years old, and it predates the recent conflicts.
  Label it.
- **The Konso Cultural Landscape** [3] — a **living community** maintaining terraced agriculture,
  fortified villages and **stele-carving practices**. **Inscribed for practices still being
  performed**, like Mali's Bandiagara ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)) and Nigeria's Osun-Osogbo
  ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)).
- **The Gedeo Cultural Landscape** [3] — sustainable **agroforestry** and megalithic ritual monuments,
  inscribed 2023. **An African agricultural system inscribed as world heritage** is worth noting: not
  a ruin, a method.
- **Harar Jugol** [3] — a sacred Muslim city with walls from the 13th–16th centuries. **Ethiopia's
  Christian and Muslim histories are both inscribed**, and any account that gives only the churches is
  incomplete — see the Adal/Harar material in [`dj-djibouti.md`](dj-djibouti.md).

🔴 **A seventeenth-century African philosopher writing in an African language in an African script.**
Zara Yaqob [1] is the entry in this whole folder that most directly refutes the assumption behind
"reclaiming African voices" — **the voice was never lost; it was not listened to.** For a project
whose four pillars are 20th-century South African writers, **Ethiopia offers a written intellectual
tradition older than the English novel.**

⚠️ **No modern Ethiopian literature is recorded here** — no literature search was run in this pass.
Amharic has a substantial modern literature `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **A real gap.**

## Heritage & sites

**Twelve UNESCO World Heritage Sites — the most of any country in this folder** [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Simien National Park | 1978 | Natural | Jagged peaks, deep valleys and precipices on the Ethiopian plateau |
| **Rock-Hewn Churches, Lalibela** | 1978 | Cultural | **Eleven monolithic churches carved in the 13th century** |
| Fasil Ghebbi, Gondar | 1979 | Cultural | Royal palace complex built 1636; Hindu, Arab and European Baroque influences |
| **Aksum** | 1980 | Cultural | Ancient capital: carved stelae, royal tombs, Red Sea trade evidence |
| **Lower Valley of the Awash** | 1980 | Cultural | **"Lucy"** and other early hominid fossils |
| Tiya | 1980 | Cultural | **36 megalithic stelae** carved with sword-like symbols |
| Lower Valley of the Omo | 1980 | Cultural | Early hominids and **some of the oldest stone tools** |
| Harar Jugol | 2006 | Cultural | Sacred Muslim city, walls of the 13th–16th centuries |
| **Konso Cultural Landscape** | 2011 | Cultural | Living terraced agriculture, fortified villages, stele-carving |
| Bale Mountains National Park | 2023 | Natural | Endemic species including the Ethiopian wolf |
| **Gedeo Cultural Landscape** | 2023 | Cultural | Agroforestry and megalithic ritual monuments |
| Melka Kunture and Balchit | 2024 | Cultural | **1.7+ million years** of deposits |

- **Ten of the twelve are cultural** [3] — the exact inverse of the DRC, where all five are natural
  ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)), and of Côte d'Ivoire, where four of five are
  ([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)). **The country that was never colonised has the
  fullest cultural heritage record on the continent.** That correlation deserves to be stated
  carefully rather than asserted as cause — but it is hard to miss.

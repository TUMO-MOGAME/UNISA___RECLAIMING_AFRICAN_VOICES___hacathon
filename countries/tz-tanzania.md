## 🔴 National anthem — Sontonga's song, in Swahili

- **Title (own language):** ***Mungu ibariki Afrika*** (Swahili)
- **Title (English):** **"God Bless Africa"**
- **Adopted:** **9 December 1961** — Tanganyika's independence day
- **Words:** collectively translated — **Sontonga's original Xhosa lyrics adapted into Swahili**
- **Music by:** **Enoch Mankayi Sontonga**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> 🔴 **Read this next to what the app already ships.** Source [2]:
>
> - The composer is **Enoch Mankayi Sontonga** — **the Xhosa composer of *Nkosi Sikelel' iAfrika***.
> - **Tanzania was the first African nation to adopt this song as its national anthem**, in 1961, while
>   still called Tanganyika, and kept it after the 1964 union with Zanzibar.
> - The Swahili version **"differs from Sontonga's original Xhosa composition and was created through
>   collaborative translation efforts."**
> - The song **"has since become influential across the continent, later adopted by South Africa and
>   Zambia with modified lyrics."**
>
> **One song, written by a Xhosa schoolteacher, is the national anthem of at least three African
> countries in three languages.** Ubuntu Heritage's four pillars are South African writers; **this is a
> South African composer whose work became continental infrastructure** — and, per source [2],
> **Tanzania adopted it before South Africa did.**
>
> **For a project called *Reclaiming African Voices*, this is close to a perfect object**: an African
> composition that crossed borders and languages under its own power, was translated collaboratively
> rather than imposed, and is sung by millions of people who do not speak the language it was written
> in. **If one anthem recording is sourced for `/countries`, it should be this one**, and it should be
> presented alongside South Africa's.
>
> ⚠️ Verify the details against a source stronger than [2] before shipping — **especially the claim
> that Tanzania was first**, which is checkable and consequential. Also cross-check
> [`zm-zambia.md`](zm-zambia.md), whose anthem source [2] says uses the same melody.

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The Maji Maji rebellion: 300,000 deaths, 1905–07** [1]. Source [1] states it in one line. **This
  is one of the largest anti-colonial death tolls in African history and it is barely known outside the
  region.** It belongs with the Congo Free State ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)) and Namibia's
  German colonial history in any honest account of German rule in Africa. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — and it
  needs a good one.
- 🔴 ***Ujamaa* is a Swahili word for an African political philosophy** [1] — like *ubuntu*, which
  this app is named after. **Nyerere built a national programme on a concept-word from his own
  language rather than translating one in.** For a project named **Ubuntu Heritage**, that parallel is
  worth drawing explicitly. ⚠️ *Ujamaa*'s outcomes were mixed and it is contested `[NEEDS SOURCE]` —
  say so; the project's rule is to keep the complication.
- ⚠️ **Zanzibar as the centre of the East African slave trade, 1840** [1]. **This is the folder's
  clearest statement of the Indian Ocean slave system** — the one Mauritius, Seychelles, Comoros and
  Mozambique all point at and none documents
  ([`mu-mauritius.md`](mu-mauritius.md), [`sc-seychelles.md`](sc-seychelles.md),
  [`km-comoros.md`](km-comoros.md), [`mz-mozambique.md`](mz-mozambique.md)). **Five files now converge
  on Zanzibar. That is where the research should start.**
- **Olduvai Gorge** [1] belongs with Ethiopia's Awash and Omo valleys
  ([`et-ethiopia.md`](et-ethiopia.md)) — **the deep human record is an East African story spanning at
  least two of these files.**
- **Two independence dates and a union by choice** [1] — like Somalia's two Somalilands
  ([`so-somalia.md`](so-somalia.md)) and unlike Senegal and Mali, whose federation broke
  ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md), [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)).

## Literature & voices

⚠️ **Source [1] names no Tanzanian writer at all**, and no literature search was run in this pass.
**A gap in the research, not a fact about the country** — and a conspicuous one, because **Swahili has
a written literary tradition going back centuries** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, including the classical
**utenzi** verse form.

The one literary figure this file does hold is **Enoch Sontonga** [2] — **a South African**, whose song
Tanzania sings.

🔴 **Swahili literature is the priority gap in the whole Eastern African set.** A language with an
official standing across four countries, a centuries-old written canon, and this app has neither the
language nor a single work recorded.

**Official-language instrument:** **the 2023 constitution**, approved by **97% in a referendum**, which
**removed French as the official language and adopted thirteen national languages** [1].
`[NEEDS SOURCE]` for the article number — but the instrument itself is identified, and this is the
most consequential language law in the folder.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all thirteen.

🔴 **Why this is the most important language finding in `countries/`.** Ubuntu Heritage exists because
South Africa's Constitution named eleven languages in 1996 and the app tries to honour that. **In June
2023 Mali went further than any country in this folder: it *removed* the coloniser's language from
official status and put thirteen African languages in its place** [1].

That is not a symbolic gesture in a preamble — it is the legal architecture this entire project argues
for, enacted three years ago, and **nothing in the app reflects it**. Compare:

- **Guinea** — nine official languages, eight indigenous, *including* French ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md))
- **Ghana** — English official, ten sponsored ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md))
- **Mali** — **thirteen national languages, French demoted**

⚠️ **And it must be told with its context.** The 2023 constitution came from a **military government**
that took power in coups in 2020 and 2021, and by 2025 had **dissolved political parties and granted
Assimi Goïta a five-year renewable term without elections** [1]. Language decolonisation and
democratic erosion happened in the same document and the same period. **Ubuntu Heritage must not
celebrate the first while hiding the second** — that would be exactly the flattening this project
refuses elsewhere.

## National anthem

- **Title:** *Le Mali* (French) — "Mali"
- **Adopted:** 9 August 1962
- **Words by:** **Seydou Badian Kouyaté** — described by source [2] as a prominent writer and politician
- **Music by:** **Banzoumana Sissoko**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The anthem it replaced may have been older than the country by seven centuries.** Source [2]
> records that the **Mali Federation** (with Senegal) had used music "**based on an old folk tune,
> possibly dating from the Mali Empire of the 13th century**". Recorded here with source [2]'s own
> hedge — "possibly" — intact. If it can be confirmed, it is remarkable: a modern state briefly
> singing a melody from the empire it is named after.
>
> **Banzoumana Sissoko** was a Malian musician of major standing `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — and the anthem
> being composed by a traditional musician rather than a European bandmaster is worth noting against
> Morocco's Léo Morgan ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)) and Guinea-Bissau's Xiao He
> ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)).

modern borders.

| 1591 | The Moroccan invasion | The Songhai Empire collapses. | [1] |
| 1905 | French control firm | The territory becomes French Sudan. | [1] |
| 1960 (20 Jun) | Independence as the **Mali Federation** | With Senegal. | [1] |
| **1960 (22 Sep)** | **The Republic of Mali** | After Senegal withdraws; this is Independence Day. | [1] |
| 1991 | The March Revolution | Moussa Traoré overthrown; multi-party democracy established. | [1] |
| 2012 | Tuareg rebellion, coup, **Azawad declared** | | [1] |
| 2020 · 2021 | Two coups | Assimi Goïta becomes interim president. | [1] |
| **2023 (Jun)** | **The new constitution** | French removed as official; thirteen national languages adopted. | [1] |
| 2025 | Parties dissolved | Goïta granted a five-year renewable term without elections. | [1] |

**Framing notes.**

- **Kirina (1235) is shared with Guinea** ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)) and the **Mali Empire is
  shared with Senegal, Guinea, The Gambia and Guinea-Bissau**. Write it once, across the files, or the
  folder will contain five competing national claims to one empire.
- **"The wealthiest country in Africa" at 1300** [1] is the single most useful sentence for correcting
  what a reader expects. Timbuktu was a university city while much of Europe was not.
- ⚠️ **2012 and the manuscripts.** The Timbuktu manuscripts were threatened during the 2012 conflict
  and large numbers were evacuated by librarians `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. If that holds it is one of the
  great preservation stories of this century and it belongs in this project. **Verify before use.**
- **Independence Day is the day the federation broke, not the day the French left** [1]. A small,
  telling fact about how these borders were made.

- **Griots** — source [1] documents the griot tradition among the Fula, with photography from 1890,
  as oral historical transmission. This is the same institution as The Gambia's *jali*
  ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)) and it is the living form of the **Sundiata epic**, the founding
  narrative of the empire begun at Kirina. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for the epic itself — and it is overdue,
  because it is one of the world's major oral literatures and this folder still has no proper entry
  for it.
- **The Dogon** of the Bandiagara escarpment [3] have among the most studied cosmologies in
  anthropology — and that literature is itself contested `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. Handle carefully: this is
  precisely where outside scholarship has previously invented what it claimed to record.
- **Senufo and Bobo appear in Burkina Faso's tables too** ([`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md)),
  and **Tamasheq/Tuareg** connect to Libya, Algeria and Niger.

### 🔴 The sentence this whole project could be built on

> **"En Afrique, quand un vieillard meurt, c'est une bibliothèque qui brûle."**
> *"In Africa, when an old man dies, it is a library that burns."*
>
> — **Amadou Hampâté Bâ**, speaking as **head of Mali's delegation to the UNESCO General Conference,
> 1 December 1960** [4]

**This is Ubuntu Heritage's thesis, said by a Malian at the UN, sixty-six years ago.** The Living
Archive — *Mantswe a Batho*, recording elders' voices before they are lost — is a direct answer to
that sentence. The app should quote it, attribute it precisely, and say where it was said.

⚠️ **Attribute it carefully.** The phrase circulates widely as an anonymous "African proverb", which
is exactly the erasure this project exists to reverse: **a named Malian intellectual's argument,
turned into folklore with no author.** Source [4] includes a proverb-etymology page tracking that
drift. Giving Hampâté Bâ his name back is a small act of the thing the app is for.

**And note what he actually did**, not just what he said: from the 1940s, with Théodore Monod's
support, he joined **IFAN in Dakar** and set out to **collect, transcribe and analyse the oral
traditions of West Africa** [4]. He is the direct precursor of Credo Mutwa in this project's canon and
of Liberia's Bai T. Moore ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)) — and, unlike either, he took the argument
to a world body and won it a hearing.

**The Timbuktu manuscripts are the other half of the answer.** Close to a million manuscripts, on
astronomy and mathematics, in Sudani script [1]. Africa's knowledge was **both** oral and written, at
scale, centuries ago. Any framing that treats African tradition as *only* oral is repeating a colonial
error — and this file contains the evidence against it.

## Heritage & sites

Four UNESCO World Heritage Sites — **three of them in danger** [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | In danger | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Old Towns of Djenné** | 1988 | Cultural | **Yes** | Mud-built architecture; **inhabited since 250 BC** |
| **Timbuktu** | 1988 | Cultural | **Yes** | Intellectual capital; three great mosques and a historic Koranic university from Islam's golden age |
| **Cliff of Bandiagara (Land of the Dogons)** | 1989 | **Mixed** | **Yes** | Geology, Dogon architecture and **living cultural practices** |
| **Tomb of Askia** | 2004 | Cultural | No | A **17-metre pyramidal structure built in 1495** by Songhai Emperor Askia Mohamed; West African mud-building at its height |

Fifteen further properties are on the tentative list [3].

- **Bandiagara is inscribed as "Mixed" partly for *living cultural practices*** [3] — heritage that is
  still being performed, not only preserved. That is the category Ubuntu Heritage's Living Archive
  belongs to.
- **The mud architecture of Djenné, the Tomb of Askia and Côte d'Ivoire's Sudanese-style mosques
  ([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)) are one building tradition across three files.**
- **Three of four in danger** — the same profile as Libya ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)), and for
  related reasons.

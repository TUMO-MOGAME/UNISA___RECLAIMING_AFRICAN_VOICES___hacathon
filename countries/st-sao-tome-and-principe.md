**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all four.

🔴 **Three creoles in a country of 220,372 people** [1]. This is the densest concentration of creole
languages in the folder, and it is a direct product of the history below: **the islands were
uninhabited before 1470** [1], so **every language spoken here today arrived with the people who were
brought or came** — and three of the four were made here.

**The folder's creole count is now nine**: Forro, Angolar and Principense here; Krio
([`sl-sierra-leone.md`](sl-sierra-leone.md)), Kriol
([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)), Kriolu ([`cv-cabo-verde.md`](cv-cabo-verde.md)),
Sango ([`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)), Kituba
([`cg-congo-republic.md`](cg-congo-republic.md)) and Annobonese
([`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md)). **Only one of the nine — Sango — holds full
official status.** That is a finding about how African languages are ranked in law, and it is now
evidenced across nine countries.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *Independência total* (Portuguese)
- **Title (English):** **"Total Independence"**
- **Adopted:** 1975
- **Words by:** **Alda Neves da Graça do Espírito Santo**
- **Music by:** Manuel dos Santos Barreto de Sousa e Almeida
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> 🔴 **The only anthem in this folder written by a woman.** Source [2] records that Alda Neves da
> Graça do Espírito Santo went on to be **minister of Education and Culture**, **minister of
> Information and Culture**, **president of the National Assembly**, and **general secretary of the
> National Union of Writers and Artists of São Tomé e Príncipe**.
>
> Every other named anthem lyricist in this research is a man — Senghor
> ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)), Cabral ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)), Boganda
> ([`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)), Warner
> ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)), Damas ([`ga-gabon.md`](ga-gabon.md)) and the rest. **This
> matters for a project whose flagship text, *Mhudi*, exists because Plaatje put a woman at the centre
> of a war story.** São Tomé's founding national text was written by one.
>
> **"Total Independence"** is also the most direct anthem title in the folder — no metaphor, no
> forefathers, no soil. Compare Guinea's *Liberté* ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)) and Benin's
> *L'Aube Nouvelle* ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)).

## Milestones

| Year | Milestone | One grounded line | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1470 (21 Dec)** | **The Portuguese arrive** | **The islands "were uninhabited when the Portuguese arrived."** | [1] |
| 1493 | The first successful settlement | Under Álvaro Caminha; the early settlers were largely "undesirables" sent from Portugal, **including Sephardic Jews**. | [1] |
| **1515** | **Slave depots** | A water-powered sugar mill is introduced and the islands "had become slave depots for the coastal slave trade". | [1] |
| mid-16th c. | **Africa's foremost sugar exporter** | Sugar plantations worked by enslaved labour. | [1] |
| **1549** | **Two maroons win a declaration of freedom** | They petitioned for it and received it. | [1] |
| **1595** | **Amador's rebellion** | It **destroyed 60 of the island's 85 sugar mills**. | [1] |
| **1905** | **The world's largest producer of cocoa** | | [1] |
| **1953** | **The Batepá Massacre** | Riots break out; **"several hundred African labourers were killed."** | [1] |
| **1975 (12 Jul)** | **Independence from Portugal** | | [1] |
| 1990 | Constitutional reform | Multi-party democracy. | [1] |
| 2021 | Carlos Vila Nova elected president | | [1] |

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **This country exists because of the Atlantic slave trade, and there is no earlier layer to
  return to.** Source [1]: the islands were **uninhabited** in 1470 and were **slave depots by 1515**.
  Almost every other file in this folder can set a precolonial African past against a colonial
  rupture. **São Tomé cannot.** Its people, its languages, its cuisine and its music were all made in
  and after that rupture — and are no less African for it.
  **For a project called *Reclaiming African Voices*, that is a genuinely difficult and important
  case**, because it refuses the idea that authenticity means "before the Europeans came". Handle it
  as the country's own creation story, not as a deficit.
- 🔴 **1549 and 1595 are the two dates that matter most.** Two maroons **petitioning for and winning a
  declaration of freedom** in 1549, and **Amador's rebellion destroying 60 of 85 sugar mills** in 1595
  [1] — enslaved people using both law and force, in the sixteenth century, and leaving a record of
  it. **This is resistance history two centuries older than most of what this folder records**, and it
  is almost entirely unknown. **Amador deserves a proper source and a place in the app.**
- ⚠️ **The Batepá Massacre, 1953** [1] — "several hundred African labourers were killed", in a
  Portuguese colony, within living memory. It belongs beside Pidjiguiti in Guinea-Bissau (1959,
  [`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)) and Sharpeville (1960) — **three massacres of unarmed
  African workers within seven years, in three different empires**, each of which hardened a
  liberation movement. **Ubuntu Heritage already tells Sharpeville. This is the continental context
  for it.**
- **1905's cocoa** [1] is the plantation economy that Batepá grew out of, and the *roças* worked it
  with contract labour that was slavery under another name `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

## Peoples & cultures

- **Population 220,372 (2023)** [1] — the smallest country in this folder so far.
- **"Predominantly of African and *mestiço* (mixed European and African) descent, with most
  practising Christianity"** [1]. **No percentages are given and none should be invented** — for a
  creole population this is exactly where ancestry figures become race science, the risk
  [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md) already refused over the admixture table.
- **The Angolares** — the Angolar Creole speakers [1] — are, by widespread account, descended from
  maroons `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, which would connect the 1549 and 1595 events directly to a language still
  spoken today. **Worth real research: a living language descended from a community of escaped
  enslaved people is exactly the kind of survival this project exists to name.**

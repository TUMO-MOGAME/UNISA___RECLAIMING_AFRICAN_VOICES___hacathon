**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all four.

⚠️ **Both official languages come from outside; both national languages are the country's own** [1] —
and the national ones are what the 95% Somali and Afar population speaks (see below). This is the same
two-tier arrangement as Senegal ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)) and Mauritania
([`mr-mauritania.md`](mr-mauritania.md)), and the inverse of Burundi, where the indigenous language is
official *and* sole national ([`bi-burundi.md`](bi-burundi.md)).

**Somali and Afar both cross borders** — Somali into Somalia ([`so-somalia.md`](so-somalia.md)),
Ethiopia and Kenya; Afar into Ethiopia and Eritrea. **Djibouti is a small state carved out of two
large language regions**, which is what its former name recorded: the **French Territory of the Afars
and the Issas** [1] named the two peoples rather than a place.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ***Jabuuti*** (Somali)
- **Title (English):** "Djibouti"
- **Adopted:** 1977
- **Words by:** Aden Elmi
- **Music by:** Abdi Robleh
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The anthem is in Somali** [2] — a national language, not one of the two official ones. **A country
> whose official languages are Arabic and French sings its anthem in the language its people
> actually speak.** That is a rare case in this folder of the anthem being *more* indigenous than the
> law.
>
> Source [2] records the lyrics working through the flag's colours — "the everlasting green of the
> earth, the blue of the sky, and white, the colour of peace" and "the red star of blood".

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The Land of Punt in the 25th century BC** [1] is the oldest dated event in this entire folder
  after the Bouar Megaliths ([`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)) and
  Chad's 7th-millennium settlement ([`td-chad.md`](td-chad.md)). **Punt is where Egypt sent
  expeditions to trade** — meaning this coast appears in Egyptian records as a **partner**, not a
  periphery. Set beside [`eg-egypt.md`](eg-egypt.md), it corrects the assumption that ancient Egypt
  faced only north.
  ⚠️ **Punt's location is disputed among Egyptologists** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — several countries claim
  it. Say that it is contested rather than placing it definitively.
- **This is the folder's first substantial Islamic political history** — Adal, Zeila, Harar, Aussa,
  the *Futuh al-Habash* [1] — a sequence of sultanates and imamates spanning seven centuries. It is
  **shared with Ethiopia, Eritrea and Somalia**, and 1403 and 1529–43 are episodes of a long conflict
  between Christian Ethiopia and its Muslim neighbours. **These files must be written consistently
  and none of them should tell it as a one-sided story.**
- **Three colonial names in 115 years** [1], the last naming the two peoples rather than the land.
  Compare Upper Volta → Burkina Faso ([`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md)) and Zaire → DRC
  ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)) — but note the difference: **those were renamings by
  Africans; these were renamings by France.**

## Peoples & cultures

| Group | Share | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Somalis** | **60%** | [1] |
| **Afar** | **35%** | [1] |
| Arabs | 5% | [1] |

- **Two peoples are 95% of the country** [1], and both extend well beyond its borders. The
  colonial-era name — the Territory of the Afars and the Issas — recorded that directly.
- ⚠️ **The Somali and Afar are both traditionally pastoral peoples with strong oral traditions**
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Somali oral poetry is among the most developed in the world** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`
  and is flagged again in [`so-somalia.md`](so-somalia.md) — **research it once and use it in both
  files.** For a project about oral inheritance, this is one of the most promising leads in Eastern
  Africa.

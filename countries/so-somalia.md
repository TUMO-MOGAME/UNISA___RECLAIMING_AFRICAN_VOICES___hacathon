### The 1972 orthography decision

Source [4] records that the **Somali Latin alphabet was officially adopted on 21 October 1972**, by
the Supreme Revolutionary Council, **as part of elevating Somali to the nation's sole official
language**. It was **developed by Shire Jama Ahmed** and **selected from among 18 competing
orthographies**. Before the twentieth century, **Somali was written in the Arabic script** [4].

🔴 **Eighteen competing ways to write one African language, and a state choosing between them.** That
is not a country receiving literacy — **it is a country deciding, by deliberation, how its own
language would be written**, and doing it in 1972.

**And one of the rejected candidates was an indigenous invention.** Source [4] names the **Osmanya
script** as a Somali writing system distinct from the Latin one `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for its origin —
**which would make it the fourth indigenous African script in this folder**, after Ge'ez
([`et-ethiopia.md`](et-ethiopia.md)), Vai ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)) and N'Ko
([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)).

| Script | Where | Status in this research |
|---|---|---|
| **Ge'ez / Ethiopic** | Ethiopia, Eritrea | Attested 3rd c. AD; in official use |
| **Vai** | Liberia | c. 1833; in use |
| **N'Ko** | Guinea | 1949; in use |
| **Osmanya** | Somalia | ⚠️ `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — verify |

**Liberia's file records a claim that at least 27 scripts have been invented for West African
languages since the 1830s** ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)). **Somalia extends that story to the
Horn.** This is now unambiguously a `docs/` document rather than a scattering of research notes.

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **"The Dervish movement defeated by British airpower", 1920** [1]. Source [1] states it in six
  words. **This was one of the first uses of aerial bombardment against an anti-colonial movement**
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — and the movement's leader, **Sayyid Mohammed Abdullah Hassan, was himself a poet**
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, which if confirmed makes him the most literal possible instance of this project's
  subject: **a poet leading a twenty-year resistance, ended by aircraft.** **Verify. This is the
  single most valuable unconfirmed lead in the Eastern African files.**
- **Punt at 2350 BC** [1] — and Djibouti's file dates the earliest Egyptian expedition to the 25th
  century BC ([`dj-djibouti.md`](dj-djibouti.md)). ⚠️ **Punt's location is contested and more than one
  country claims it** — say it is disputed rather than placing it.
- **Somalia is two colonies joined by choice** [1] — British and Italian Somaliland unified within
  five days in 1960. **Almost every other border in this folder was drawn by Europeans and kept;
  this one was drawn by Europeans and *undone* by Somalis.** ⚠️ The relationship between Somalia and
  Somaliland today is contested `[NEEDS SOURCE]` and must not be characterised from this file's
  sources.
- **1104–1105 as "the oldest writing in Somalia"** [1] — nine centuries before the 1972 alphabet.

## Peoples & cultures

**Source [1] (2021):** around **85% ethnic Somalis**, **15% other**.

- **Somali populations extend into four other countries in this folder** — Djibouti (60%,
  [`dj-djibouti.md`](dj-djibouti.md)), Ethiopia (6.2%, [`et-ethiopia.md`](et-ethiopia.md)), Kenya
  (5.85%, [`ke-kenya.md`](ke-kenya.md)). **Somali is one of the most widely distributed single
  languages in the region**, and it was divided between British, Italian, French and Ethiopian
  administrations.

### 🔴 The oral poetry

Source [4] names the forms directly, and they are not a single genre but a **system**:

| Form | What it is |
|---|---|
| **gabay** | Usually chanted — the major form |
| **jiifto** | Chanted; usually moody |
| **geeraar** | Short; deals with war |
| **buraambur** | **Composed by women** |
| **heello / balwo** | Short love poems, **popular on the radio** |
| **hees** | Popular poetry |

**A named, differentiated poetic system with a form specifically composed by women (*buraambur*) and
a form that moved onto radio (*heello*)** [4]. **This is the most detailed oral-literary tradition
documented anywhere in this folder** — more so than the griot material still outstanding in The
Gambia, Mali and Senegal ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md), [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md),
[`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)).

**And note *heello*: an oral form that adapted to broadcast media** [4]. **That is precisely what
Ubuntu Heritage is attempting** — carrying an oral form into a new medium without killing it. Somalia
did it with radio.

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Shire Jama Ahmed** | **the Somali Latin orthography** | **1972** | for Somali | [4] |
| **Nuruddin Farah** | *From a Crooked Rib* | **1970** | English | [4] |
| Nuruddin Farah | *Sweet and Sour Milk* | **1979** | English | [4] |
| Nuruddin Farah | *Maps* | **1986** | English | [4] |
| Nuruddin Farah | *Gifts* | **1993** | English | [4] |
| Nuruddin Farah | *Secrets* | **1998** | English | [4] |

🔴 **Source [4] on Nuruddin Farah is the closest thing to a mission statement this research has
found.**

> **Farah's mother, Aleeli, was an oral poet.** His **"novelistic prose style is rooted in the
> tradition of oral Somali poetry, including the use of alliteration, the hallmark of Somali oral
> verse, as well as symbolism and imagery from the oral poetic tradition."** [4]

**A novelist whose sentences are built out of his mother's poems.** Not a writer who *collected* oral
tradition (Bai T. Moore in Liberia, [`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md); Hampâté Bâ in Mali,
[`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)), and not one who *adapted* a tale (Sutherland's *Anansewa* in Ghana,
[`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md); Tutuola in Nigeria, [`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) — **one whose
prose technique itself is inherited from an oral form, through a named woman.**

**That completes a pattern this folder has been assembling country by country**: Craveirinha putting
African rhythms into Portuguese ([`mz-mozambique.md`](mz-mozambique.md)), Kourouma breaking French
([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)), Silá and Semedo bending Portuguese
([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)), Vilakazi and European metre. **Six countries, one
method.** Ubuntu Heritage should name it in the submission narrative — and Farah is the clearest case
because the transmission is documented down to a single person.

⚠️ **Everything Farah published is in English** [4] — while his country was, in the same decade,
deciding how to write Somali. **Both facts are true and the tension between them is the subject.**

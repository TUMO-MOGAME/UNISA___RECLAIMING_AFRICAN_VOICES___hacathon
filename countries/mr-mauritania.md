**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all four.

**One official language, three recognised national ones — and the split is ethnic.** Arabic is the
language of the Bidhan and, by imposition, of the state; Pulaar, Soninke and Wolof are the languages
of the sub-Saharan third of the population [1]. In a country where the same fault line runs through
the slavery question below, **language policy here is not a neutral administrative fact** and should
never be presented as one. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for the Arabisation policy specifically — but do not write
this section up as a simple list until that is in hand.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** نشيد وطني موريتاني
- **Title (English):** "National Anthem of Mauritania"
- **Adopted:** **28 November 2017** — replacing the anthem in use since 1960
- **Words by:** unknown [2]
- **Music by:** Rajih Sami Daoud — **an Egyptian composer** [2]
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The newest anthem in this folder.** Mauritania replaced its national anthem by **referendum in
> August 2017**, with the change taking effect on independence day that November [2]. Most countries
> here changed their anthem after a coup or a revolution; Mauritania put it to a vote.
>
> Compare Libya ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)), Tunisia ([`tn-tunisia.md`](tn-tunisia.md)) and Ghana
> ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)), whose anthems changed with governments. **Four of the West and North
> African countries researched so far have altered their national song within living memory** — the
> app should never present an anthem as a fixed national inheritance.

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **"Slavery officially abolished in 1981 and 2007 (also 1905)"** [1]. **Three abolitions is not a
  clerical curiosity — it is the evidence that the first two did not work.** Mauritania is widely
  described as the last country in the world to abolish slavery, and 2007 is the year it was made a
  **crime** rather than merely illegal. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for both of those characterisations, and they
  must be sourced properly before use.
  **Compare The Gambia's 1906** ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)) and **Botswana's *botlhanka***
  ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)). This project has already committed to telling hereditary
  servitude honestly where it existed within African societies. **Mauritania is the hardest instance
  and the most recent, and it is not optional.**
- ⚠️ **It is a live human-rights question, not settled history.** Anything the app says must be
  written in the knowledge that this concerns people alive now, and it should be reviewed by someone
  with standing to review it — the same rule the project applies to *botlhanka*.
- **The Char Bouba War (1644–74)** [1] is a rare documented case of a thirty-year indigenous
  resistance to *Arab* conquest, which complicates any simple "Africa vs Europe" framing of this
  continent's history. Keep it.
- **Kumbi Saleh — on the tentative heritage list** [3] — is by widespread account the capital of the
  **Ghana Empire** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. If so, the empire named in [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md) has its
  capital in this country, and neither modern Ghana nor modern Mali is where it was.

## Peoples & cultures

| Group | Share | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Haratin** | **40%** | [1] |
| **Bidhan (Beidane)** | **30%** | [1] |
| Sub-Saharan groups — Halpulaar, Fulani, Soninké, Wolof | **30%** | [1] |

🔴 **This table cannot be used without explanation.** The **Haratin** are, by widespread account, the
descendants of enslaved people held by the Bidhan `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — which means the largest group in
Mauritania is defined by that history, and the abolition dates above are about them. **Publishing
these percentages as neutral demography would be the single most irresponsible thing this folder could
do.** Either the file explains what the categories mean, with a proper source, or it does not use them
at all.

- **The ksour** — Ouadane, Chinguetti, Tichitt and Oualata — "illustrate a traditional way of life
  centred on the nomadic culture" of the western Sahara [3], with houses set around courtyards and
  mosques with square minarets.
- **Chinguetti is renowned for its manuscript libraries** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — source [3] explicitly
  does *not* mention manuscripts. If it holds, Mauritania joins Mali ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)) as
  a second Saharan centre of written African scholarship, and that is worth real research effort.
- **Pulaar, Soninke and Wolof link this file to Senegal, Mali, The Gambia and Guinea.**

## Heritage & sites

Two UNESCO World Heritage Sites [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Banc d'Arguin National Park** | 1989 | **Natural** | Sand dunes, coastal swamps, small islands and shallow waters; migratory birds, sea turtles and dolphins |
| **Ancient Ksour of Ouadane, Chinguetti, Tichitt and Oualata** | 1996 | Cultural | Founded in the **11th–12th centuries** as Saharan trading centres; houses with courtyards around mosques with **square minarets** |

Three tentative sites, all listed in 2001: **Azougui Cultural Landscape**, **Kumbi Saleh** and
**Tegdaoust** [3].

- **The ksour are the trans-Saharan trade network in built form** — the same road that runs through
  Mali's Djenné and Timbuktu ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)), Burkina Faso's Loropéni
  ([`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md)) and Morocco's Ait-Ben-Haddou
  ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)). **Five files now hold pieces of one system.** Somebody should
  draw that map.

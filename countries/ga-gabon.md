**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all of them.

⚠️ **French is the only official language and none of the seven named indigenous languages has any
stated status** [1]. Gabon sits at the opposite end of the folder's spectrum from Mali, which removed
French entirely ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)), and from Guinea, which made eight indigenous languages
official ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)).

**These are Bantu languages** — source [1] states Bantu groups are **around 95% of the population** —
which places Gabon in the same broad family as the app's own eleven, along with Angola
([`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)) and the Congos. **The Bantu-speaking world is where this folder
starts to connect to what Ubuntu Heritage already ships**, and Gabon is one of its northern edges.

**Teke and Vili cross into the Congos** — the Bateke are 16.9% of the Republic of the Congo
([`cg-congo-republic.md`](cg-congo-republic.md)) — and **Fang crosses into Equatorial Guinea**, where
it is 85.7% of the population ([`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md)), **and Cameroon**.
One people across three states and two colonial languages.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *La Concorde* (French)
- **Title (English):** "The Concord"
- **Adopted:** 17 August 1960 — independence day itself
- **Words and music by:** **Georges Aleka Damas** (both)
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Written entirely by a Gabonese author** [2] — words and music both — which after Equatorial
> Guinea's Spanish army officer ([`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md)) and the
> Frenchman who scored both Senegal's and the CAR's anthems ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md),
> [`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)) is worth stating rather than
> assuming.
>
> Source [2] records that Damas "had held many diplomatic posts in Gabon since the mid 1940s, and was
> actively involved in politics and in **the formation of labour unions**". **The union thread again**
> — Côte d'Ivoire's independence movement began in a farmers' union
> ([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)) and Guinea-Bissau's turned militant after a dock
> workers' massacre ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)). **Organised labour runs through
> West and Central African independence more consistently than this folder has yet said.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **"13th century BCE — Bantu migrants began settling the area"** [1]. **This is the same migration
  the app already tells** — the Atlas material on the peopling of Southern Africa
  ([STATUS.md](../STATUS.md) records "The Peopling of SA (Khoisan → Bantu → Sotho-Tswana & Nguni)").
  **Gabon is a waypoint on that road**, three thousand years before it reached the Highveld. **This is
  the most direct link between this folder and content Ubuntu Heritage has already built**, and it
  should be used: the Journey's story does not start in South Africa.
- ⚠️ **The Bongo family held the presidency from 1967 until the 2023 coup** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — source
  [1] gives Omar Bongo's accession in 1967 and the 2023 coup, but not the succession between them.
  Fill that in before writing about Gabon's politics; a fifty-six-year family tenure is not a detail.
- **The Kingdom of Orungu (from 1700)** [1] joins Loango ([`cg-congo-republic.md`](cg-congo-republic.md))
  and Kongo ([`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)) as Atlantic-coast African states with one line each in
  this folder. ⚠️ Orungu was involved in the slave trade `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — check before presenting it
  simply as a precolonial kingdom, the same obligation as Dahomey ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)).
- **2023 puts Gabon with Niger (2023), Mali (2020, 2021) and Guinea-Bissau (2025)** as coups recorded
  in this folder within five years.

## Peoples & cultures

- **Bantu groups are around 95% of the population** [1] — and **source [1] gives no individual
  percentages at all**, naming Fang, Punu, Nzebi, Teke, Myene, Kota, Vili and Mbama without shares.
  **No figures are recorded here and none should be invented.**
- **Fang across three countries** — Gabon, Equatorial Guinea (85.7%) and Cameroon. **Fang reliquary
  sculpture is among the most influential African art in European modernism** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`;
  flagged identically in [`gq-equatorial-guinea.md`](gq-equatorial-guinea.md). **Two files now point
  at the same unresearched art-history claim** — worth one proper pass.
- ⚠️ **Bwiti** — a Gabonese spiritual tradition `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, entirely absent from source [1]. If
  covered at all it must be handled as a living religion, on the same terms as Vodun in
  [`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md) and *Modimo*/*Badimo* in [`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md) — never as
  exotica. **Do not write about it from general knowledge.**

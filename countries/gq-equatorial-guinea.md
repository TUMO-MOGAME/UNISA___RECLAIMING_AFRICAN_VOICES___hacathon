**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all of them.

🔴 **Three official languages, and all three are European.** Spanish, French and Portuguese [1] — the
only country in this folder with that arrangement, and **the only Spanish-speaking country in
Africa**. Meanwhile **85.7% of the population is Fang** [1], whose language is merely "recognised
regional".

**This is the folder's most extreme official-≠-spoken gap, measured differently from Sierra Leone's.**
Krio reaches 97% of Sierra Leone with no official status ([`sl-sierra-leone.md`](sl-sierra-leone.md));
here **three colonial languages hold official status while the language of six in seven people does
not.** Two countries, two shapes of the same erasure.

⚠️ **French and Portuguese were almost certainly adopted for regional-bloc reasons rather than because
they are spoken** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — Equatorial Guinea's neighbours are Francophone and Lusophone.
**Do not assert this without a source**, but it is the obvious question, and it would mean the country
has three official languages that most citizens do not use.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — **source [2] does not give the title in any language**
- **Adopted:** 12 October 1968 — independence day
- **Words by:** **Atanasio Ndongo Miyono**
- **Music by:** **Ramiro Sánchez López** — described by source [2] as **"a Spanish lieutenant and the
  deputy director of music at the army headquarters in Madrid"**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The music of this national anthem was written by a serving officer of the colonial power's army,
> in the colonial capital** [2]. Source [2] says so plainly — "reflecting the influence of the former
> colonial power".
>
> **This is the extreme case of a pattern the folder has now documented repeatedly.** Morocco's melody
> came from the protectorate era ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)); Senegal's and the CAR's music was
> written by a Frenchman ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md),
> [`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)); Nigeria's restored words were
> written by a British expatriate ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)); Niger rejected its anthem for
> exactly this reason ([`ne-niger.md`](ne-niger.md)). **Here it is a Spanish army officer.**
>
> ⚠️ **That the anthem's title is missing from source [2] is a gap in the research, not a fact.** Find
> it before anything ships.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **The Macías period (1968–1979) was extremely violent** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — source [1] records
  only "president for life" and the coup that ended it. This is a case where the encyclopaedia's
  neutrality understates what happened, and **Ubuntu Heritage should either source it properly or not
  cover the period at all.** A tidy list of dates here would be a false account.
- ⚠️ **Teodoro Obiang has held power since 1979** [1] — among the longest tenures of any head of state
  in the world. State it factually with the date.
- **1900's Treaty of Paris shows the borders being traded between European capitals** [1] — as at
  Berlin ([`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)) and in the 1919 partition of Cameroon
  ([`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md)).
- **Oil in 1995** [1] is the country's modern pivot and the reason its per-capita figures look unlike
  its neighbours'. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` on the distribution of that wealth — an important question this
  file cannot currently answer.

## Peoples & cultures

| Group | Share | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **Fang** | **85.7%** | [1] |
| Bubi | 6.5% | [1] |
| Ndowe | 3.6% | [1] |
| Annobon | 1.6% | [1] |
| Bujeba | 1.1% | [1] |

- **The most concentrated demographic in the folder** — 85.7% one group [1]. Compare Guinea-Bissau,
  where no group exceeds 26% ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)).
- **The Fang also live in Gabon and Cameroon** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — see [`ga-gabon.md`](ga-gabon.md).
  **Fang art, particularly reliquary figures, is among the most influential African art in European
  modernism** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — verify, because if it holds it is a restitution-and-influence story
  as significant as the Benin Bronzes ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)).
- **Annobonese Creole** [1] is a Portuguese-based creole on a small island — belonging with the
  folder's other creoles (Krio, Kriol, Kriolu, Sango, Kituba). **Six now.**
- **The Bubi are the indigenous people of Bioko** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, distinct from the mainland Fang
  majority — an island/mainland division that matters politically.

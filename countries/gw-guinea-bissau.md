**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all of them.

**The numbers here make the official/spoken gap unarguable.** **94% of the country speaks Kriol** as a
first or second language [1] — and the official language is Portuguese. That is a sharper version of
the same arrangement in Cabo Verde ([`cv-cabo-verde.md`](cv-cabo-verde.md)), and the sharpest instance
of the "official ≠ spoken" trap in the whole folder. Any Ubuntu Heritage feature that shows "official
language: Portuguese" and stops there would be describing a legal fact and hiding a national one.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *Esta é a Nossa Pátria Bem Amada* (Portuguese)
- **Title (English):** "This Is Our Beloved Country"
- **Adopted:** 24 September 1974
- **Words by:** **Amílcar Cabral** — the liberation leader himself
- **Music by:** **Xiao He**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **This anthem has the most surprising origin in the folder.** Source [2]: a **1963 delegation from
> Portuguese Guinea visited China**; after hearing the composer **Xiao He**'s work, **Amílcar Cabral
> asked him for a composition to inspire the independence struggle, drawing on African musical
> traditions**. Cabral wrote the words himself.
>
> So the national anthem of a West African country was **written by its liberation theorist and
> composed by a Chinese musician**, as an act of Cold War anti-colonial solidarity. Whatever else it
> is, it is not a colonial inheritance — compare Morocco's kept French melody
> ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)) and The Gambia's Mandinka tune under English words
> ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)).
>
> **Shared with Cabo Verde, 1975–1996.** Because Cabral's PAIGC represented both territories, the same
> anthem served both at independence; the two "even proposed to merge, but this merger dissolved
> before it was realized", and Cabo Verde adopted its own in 1996 [2]. **Guinea-Bissau kept the
> original.** Both files carry this — see [`cv-cabo-verde.md`](cv-cabo-verde.md).

**Framing notes.**

- **Amílcar Cabral is the most important person in this file and possibly in West Africa's liberation
  history.** He founded a single party for two territories [1], theorised African liberation, wrote
  his country's anthem [2] — and **was assassinated in 1973, before independence** [1]. Ubuntu
  Heritage's four South African pillars are writers; Cabral is the reminder that on this continent the
  same person was often the theorist, the organiser and the lyricist.
- ⚠️ **The Pidjiguiti massacre (1959)** is the hinge: a strike of dock workers met with killing, after
  which the movement took up arms [1]. It is the direct counterpart of **Sharpeville (1960)** in South
  Africa — a massacre of unarmed people that ended the argument for peaceful methods. **These two
  events are one year apart and the app already tells the South African one.** Pairing them would be
  among the most substantial things this research could contribute.
- ⚠️ **A coup in November 2025 is very recent** [1]. Write nothing in the present tense about
  Guinea-Bissau's government without re-checking.

| Mankanya | 3.9% |
| Biafada | 3.7% |
| Other | 6.9% |

- **No single group is a majority** — the largest is a quarter of the population. Kriol's 94% reach [1]
  is what actually holds the country together linguistically, which is a real argument for a creole as
  national infrastructure rather than a corruption of Portuguese.
- **Mandinka and Fula link this file to Guinea, Mali, Senegal and The Gambia.** The Kaabu Empire
  (1537–1867) [1] was Mandinka and covered territory now split between Guinea-Bissau, Senegal and
  Guinea.
- **The Bijagós Islands** — 88 islands, and the archipelago has its own distinct culture
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. See Heritage.

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Amílcar Cabral** (assassinated 1973) | *Unity and Struggle: Speeches and Writings* (Monthly Review Press); the national anthem's words | 1974 (anthem) | Portuguese | [4], [2] |
| **Abdulai Silá** (b. 1958, Catió) | ***Eterna Paixão*** — **the first novel published in Guinea-Bissau** | **1994** | Portuguese | [4] |
| Abdulai Silá | *A Última Tragédia* | **1995** | Portuguese | [4] |
| Abdulai Silá | *Mistida* | **1997** | Portuguese | [4] |
| **Odete Costa Semedo** (b. 1959, Bissau) | *Entre o Ser e o Amar* — poetry | **1996** | Portuguese | [4] |
| Odete Semedo | *Histórias e passadas que ouvi contar* | **2003** | Portuguese / Kriol | [4] |
| Odete Semedo | *No Fundo Do Canto* — poetry | **2007** | Portuguese | [4] |

**The first novel published in this country appeared in 1994** [4] — within living memory, twenty
years after independence. Set that beside Ghana's canon of the 1960s
([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)) and Egypt's Nobel ([`eg-egypt.md`](eg-egypt.md)) and it is a reminder
that "African literature" is not one timeline.

**And the reason the file matters to this project is what these two writers do with the language.**
Source [4] describes Semedo and Silá as **subverting Portuguese with Bissau-Guinean Crioulo
vocabulary, structures, cultural references, names and song**, and as **foregrounding the non-static
nature of stories within oral literary culture**.

That is the same technique, named in a third language: **Kourouma bending French
([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)), Silá and Semedo bending Portuguese, and Kanté in
Guinea refusing the borrowed alphabet altogether ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)).** Semedo writes in
**both Portuguese and Kriol** [4] — one of the few writers in this folder confirmed to work in the
creole itself.

## Heritage & sites

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Coastal and Marine Ecosystems of the Bijagós Archipelago – Omatí Minhô** | **2025** | Natural | The Bolama-Bijagós archipelago: **88 islands** formed from the ancient delta of the Rio Geba and Rio Grande; endangered **green and leatherback turtles, manatees, dolphins and more than 870,000 migratory birds** | [4] |

- **It failed on its first attempt in 2012 and was resubmitted after extensive scientific research and
  the involvement of local populations** [4]. **The community involvement is the part worth keeping** —
  a heritage listing won by including the people who live there is the model this project should
  point at, and it is very recent (2025).
- Bolama-Bijagós is also a **UNESCO Biosphere Reserve** and a Ramsar wetland site [4].

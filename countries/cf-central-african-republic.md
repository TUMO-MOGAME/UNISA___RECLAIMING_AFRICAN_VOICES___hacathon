## National anthem

- **Title (own languages):** *La Renaissance* (French) · ***E Zingo*** (Sango)
- **Title (English):** "The Renaissance"
- **Adopted:** 25 May 1960
- **Words by:** **Barthélemy Boganda**
- **Music by:** **Herbert Pepper**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The anthem exists in both French and Sango** [2] — the second country in this folder with two
> official anthem texts, after Cameroon's French and English ([`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md)).
> **But this pairing is different in kind**: Cameroon's two versions are both European languages;
> here one of them is African. If the app ever plays this anthem, ***E Zingo* is the version that
> serves this project's purpose**, and choosing it would be a defensible editorial act rather than a
> political one.
>
> **Herbert Pepper wrote Senegal's anthem too** [2] — and source [2] describes him as **a French
> ethnomusicologist who travelled to the CAR to research local musical traditions**. So the same
> Frenchman composed the music for two African national anthems
> ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)), in both cases setting words written by an African
> statesman-intellectual. **Worth stating plainly rather than glossing**: the words are African, the
> scores are not.
>
> **Boganda wrote the words and died before independence** [1], [2] — a plane crash on 29 March 1959,
> sixteen months before the country he was building came into being.

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The Bouar Megaliths (c. 3500–2700 BCE)** [1] are roughly contemporaneous with the Egyptian Old
  Kingdom ([`eg-egypt.md`](eg-egypt.md)) and older than almost anything else in this folder. **They
  are also almost entirely unknown outside specialist archaeology.** A megalithic culture in Central
  Africa five and a half thousand years ago is precisely the kind of fact this project exists to put
  in front of a reader — and it currently has one line from one source. **Highest-value research gap
  in this file.**
- ⚠️ **Bokassa's empire is the country's best-known fact internationally, and it is a caricature
  trap.** A self-declared emperor is the story Western media told about the CAR for decades, and
  repeating it as the headline reproduces that framing. **Boganda — elected to the French National
  Assembly in 1946, founder of a political movement, author of the national anthem, dead before he
  could govern — is the more important figure and the less told one.** If Ubuntu Heritage ever covers
  this country, lead with Boganda and the megaliths.
- **Ironwork by c. 1000 BCE** [1] belongs with the Nok culture of Nigeria (c. 1500 BC,
  [`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) and Burkina Faso's Bura culture
  ([`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md)) — the folder is accumulating an African Iron Age that
  no single file tells.

## Peoples & cultures

Source [1] names roughly **twenty ethnic groups and gives no percentages**: Baka, Banda, Bayaka, Fula,
Gula, Gbaya, Kara, Kresh, Mbaka, Mandja, Ngbandi, Runga, Sara, Vidiri, Wodaabe, Yakoma, Yulu, Kanuri
and Zande.

- ⚠️ **No figures — so invent none.** A list without shares is more honest than a table of guesses.
- **The Baka and Bayaka** are among the forest peoples referred to elsewhere by the exonym "Pygmy" —
  see the note in [`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md). **Source [1] uses their own names here**, which
  is the practice the rest of the folder should follow.
- **Gbaya, Fula, Kanuri and Sara cross into Cameroon, Chad and Nigeria** — the Central African files
  share peoples the same way the West African ones do.
- **Sango as a shared language across all of them** [1] is what makes a country of twenty-odd groups
  legible to itself. That is the argument for a lingua franca that is not the coloniser's, and it is
  the same argument Krio and Kriol make without official recognition.

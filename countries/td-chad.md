**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** both official ones, and all 200-plus indigenous ones.

⚠️ **Two official languages, both from outside, and not one indigenous language named by source [1].**
Chad is the starkest instance in the folder of a country whose own languages are invisible in its own
encyclopaedia entry. Compare Cameroon's 250 unnamed languages
([`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md)) and Nigeria's 525 ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) — but there
at least the largest were named. **Here the figure is given and the languages are not.**

**That absence is itself the finding**, and it is what [README.md](README.md)'s "official ≠ spoken"
rule is for. The honest entry for Chad, if one were ever written, would have to say: *we do not yet
know what people here speak.*

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *La Tchadienne* (French)
- **Title (English):** "The Chadian"
- **Adopted:** January 1960
- **Words by:** **Louis Gidrol and students at St Paul's School**
- **Music by:** Paul Villard
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Written by a teacher and his schoolchildren** [2]. That is the only anthem in this folder credited
> partly to students, and it is a genuinely appealing fact — but note what it also is: a **mission
> school**, in **French**, at the moment of independence. Source [2] says it calls on Chadians to build
> the nation "while honoring their ancestors".
>
> Set beside Senegal's poet-president ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)), Guinea-Bissau's liberation
> theorist ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)) and the CAR's Boganda
> ([`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)), **Chad's anthem is the one
> where the African authorial voice is least present.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **"Known from artefacts and oral histories"** [1] — source [1]'s own description of how the **Sao
  civilisation** is known. **That sentence is the entire argument of this project, written into an
  encyclopaedia entry without comment.** A civilisation whose record survives because people kept
  telling it. If Ubuntu Heritage ever needs one line to justify the Living Archive to a judge, this is
  a candidate, alongside Hampâté Bâ's ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)).
- **Kanem–Bornu is shared with Niger** ([`ne-niger.md`](ne-niger.md)), where source [1] dates it 8th–
  19th century, and reaches into Nigeria. **This folder now has the same empire in three files with
  three different framings** — it should be written once.
- **The Ennedi rock art belongs in the milestones, not just the heritage list** [3]. With Algeria's
  Tassili n'Ajjer (over 15,000 paintings, [`dz-algeria.md`](dz-algeria.md)) and Libya's Tadrart Acacus
  (12,000 BCE–100 CE, [`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)), **three files now document Saharan rock art at
  enormous scale.** That is a continuous artistic tradition across what is now three countries, made
  when the Sahara was green — and no file yet tells it as one thing. **A strong candidate for an Atlas
  entry in the app.**

## Peoples & cultures

- **Over 200 ethnic and linguistic groups** [1]. Source [1] names **Sara, Toubou, Zaghawa and
  Kanem-Bu** and **gives no percentages at all**.
- ⚠️ **No figures — invent none.**
- **The Toubou also appear in Libya's table** ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)); **Kanuri** connects to
  Niger and Nigeria. **Lake Chad** is a shared cultural landscape on Nigeria's tentative World
  Heritage list ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) — four countries meet at that lake.
- ⚠️ **Chad's north–south division** — broadly Muslim and Arabic-speaking north, broadly Christian and
  Sara south — has driven its post-independence conflicts `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. It is the same shape as
  Sudan's ([`sd-sudan.md`](sd-sudan.md)) and Togo's ([`tg-togo.md`](tg-togo.md)), and it should not be
  written up from inference.

## Heritage & sites

Two UNESCO World Heritage Sites [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lakes of Ounianga** | 2012 | Natural | **Eighteen interconnected lakes** in the hyper-arid Ennedi region of the Sahara — saline and freshwater bodies with distinctive ecosystems |
| **Ennedi Massif: Natural and Cultural Landscape** | 2016 | **Mixed** | A sandstone plateau eroded into canyons and valleys, with **thousands of images painted and carved into the rock** — one of the Sahara's largest rock-art collections |

- **Eighteen lakes in the middle of the Sahara** [3] is a fact worth using on its own — the desert as
  something other than empty.
- **Ennedi is inscribed as "Mixed"** [3], like Mali's Bandiagara ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)) and
  Algeria's Tassili n'Ajjer ([`dz-algeria.md`](dz-algeria.md)) — landscape and human record
  inseparable. **All three of those Mixed sites are in this folder and all three carry rock art or
  living practice.**

**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — source [1] names both official languages but not

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** *all of them* — Modern Standard Arabic, Tamazight,
Algerian Arabic, French.

**Same blocker as Burkina Faso.** The app speaks none of these, and
[`country-languages.ts`](../app/src/content/country-languages.ts) cannot express that: `lead` is a
required `LangCode` from the eleven South African languages. See the blocker note in
[`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages) — it governs this file too, and most of the 54.

**Worth naming: French is the colonial language that stayed.** Source [1] is explicit that French has
*no* official status yet remains in administration and education. That gap between law and daily life
is the "official ≠ spoken" trap from [README.md](README.md), in its sharpest form on this continent.

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** قسما — *Qassaman*
- **Title (English):** "We Pledge"
- **Adopted:** 5 July 1962 — independence day itself
- **Words by:** Mufdi Zakaria (موفدي زكرياء)
- **Music by:** Mohamed Fawzi (محمد فوزي)
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The detail worth keeping.** Source [2] records that Zakaria wrote the words in **1956, while
> imprisoned by the French colonial authorities in Serkaji-Barberousse Prison in Algiers**, and that
> the poem was set to music first by Mohamed Triki in 1956 and then by the Egyptian composer Mohamed
> Fawzi. A national anthem written in a colonial prison cell is the kind of fact this project exists
> to carry.

## Peoples & cultures

- **Arab and Amazigh (Berber)** — source [1] says most Algerians identify as Arab and that Berbers are
  a sizeable minority. **No percentages are stated and none should be invented.**
- **The Ibadites of the M'Zab** — five fortified villages founded between 1012 and 1350, still
  standing and inhabited. See Heritage below. [3]
- `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — Tuareg of the Algerian Sahara, oral tradition, music (raï, chaabi, Kabyle song).

**The connection worth making.** Source [4] describes *Nedjma* as arguably the most important novel in
the Francophone Maghrebi tradition, and Djebar's work as concerned with giving Algerian women the
voice "elements in society would have them denied". That is the same project as *Mhudi* — a
foundational national novel written in the coloniser's language, by choice and under pressure — and
it is the nearest thing on this continent to Plaatje's position. If Ubuntu Heritage ever reaches
beyond South Africa's four pillars, this is a door.

⚠️ **Written in French, and that is the point.** All four modern writers above wrote in the language
of the coloniser. Do not present that as a failure of authenticity — the literature argues with it
directly, and saying so carelessly would repeat a judgement Algerians have spent seventy years
refusing.

## Heritage & sites

Seven UNESCO World Heritage Sites, all from source [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Al Qal'a of Beni Hammad | 1980 | Cultural | Founded by the Hammadid dynasty in 1007, in a mountain setting |
| Tassili n'Ajjer | 1982 | **Mixed** | A sandstone plateau carrying **over 15,000 rock paintings and carvings** |
| M'Zab Valley | 1982 | Cultural | Five fortified villages founded 1012–1350 by Ibadites |
| Djémila | 1982 | Cultural | Roman town established in the late 1st century under Nerva |
| Tipasa | 1982 | Cultural | Founded as a Punic trading post in the 6th century BCE |
| Timgad | 1982 | Cultural | Founded as a Roman military colony by Trajan in 100 |
| Kasbah of Algiers | 1992 | Cultural | Mediterranean fortified citadel, Ottoman-era architecture |

**Tassili n'Ajjer is the one to look at first** — 15,000 rock paintings is a library, and it is the
oldest thing in this file by a wide margin.

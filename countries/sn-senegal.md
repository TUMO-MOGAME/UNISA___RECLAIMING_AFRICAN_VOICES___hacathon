> **How this file is put together.** Built **claim by claim**: every row names the source it came
> from, and the numbered list at the bottom is what those names point at. Sources are
> encyclopaedia-grade — rough sources are fine at this stage ([README.md](README.md)), assertions made

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all of them.

**The 80% figure is the whole argument** [1]. Wolof reaches four in five Senegalese; French is the
official language. This is the same two-tier arrangement as Cabo Verde
([`cv-cabo-verde.md`](cv-cabo-verde.md)) and Guinea-Bissau ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)),
and the app's own precedent — Botswana leads with **Setswana, the national language, not English the
official one** ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)) — says that if `sn` were ever wired, **Wolof would
lead**. The app does not have Wolof.

**Six national languages beside one official one** puts Senegal in the same bracket as Ghana's ten
sponsored languages ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)) and short of Mali's thirteen
([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md)) — a West African pattern of states formally naming their own languages
without displacing French. Mali and Niger have now gone further; Senegal has not.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## 🔴 National anthem — written by the poet-president, and it names the instruments

- **Title (own language):** *Pincez Tous vos Koras, Frappez les Balafons* (French)
- **Title (English):** **"Pluck Your Koras, Strike the Balafons"**
- **Adopted:** 1960
- **Words by:** **Léopold Sédar Senghor** — Senegal's first president
- **Music by:** Herbert Pepper, a French composer
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **A national anthem that opens by naming two African instruments** — the **kora** and the
> **balafon** — is doing something almost no other anthem in this folder does. Source [2] notes the
> composition "references traditional African instruments integral to Senegalese culture". Most
> anthems here invoke soil, blood or God; Senegal's invokes **music itself**, and specifically the
> griot's instrument.
>
> **The Mali Federation left its mark on the music.** Source [2]: at independence Senegal and French
> Sudan shared an anthem in the Mali Federation; when it dissolved after two months **Mali kept the
> original composition**, so Senegal needed new music — Pepper wrote it, and **Senghor's lyrics,
> slightly modified, were carried over**. See [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md), whose file records the same
> split from the other side.
>
> ⚠️ **The words are African and the music is French** [2] — the mirror image of The Gambia, where a
> Mandinka melody carries English words ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)). Two neighbours, opposite
> halves borrowed.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **Gorée.** Source [3] inscribes the island as a **"slave-trading centre on the African coast
  during the Atlantic slave trade period"** — plain language, unlike UNESCO's "fortified trading-posts"
  for Ghana's forts ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)). Gorée is one of the most visited sites of return
  for the African diaspora. **The scale of what passed through Gorée specifically is contested among
  historians** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`; the app should say what the island *means* and what UNESCO says,
  and not put a number on it without real scholarship.
- **Senghor retired.** [1] A head of state who left office voluntarily in 1980, when almost none of
  his contemporaries did, and went back to being a poet. That belongs in any account of him.
- **The Mali Federation is the clearest case in the folder of borders that could have been otherwise.**
  Senegal and Mali were one country for eighteen months. Two of this folder's fifty-four files describe
  one state that existed within living memory.

- **Wolof is 39.7% of the population and 80% of its speech** [1]. A language that has spread far beyond

  mandating it, which is the opposite of how French got there.
- **Every group in this table appears in at least one neighbouring file** — Wolof, Fula, Serer,
  Mandinka, Jola and Soninke all recur in [`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md),
  [`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md), [`mr-mauritania.md`](mr-mauritania.md) and
  [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md). **Senegambia is one cultural region cut by three or four borders.**

  is documented in [`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md) and [`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md) and is still
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]` across all three. **This folder now has three separate files pointing at the same
  missing research.**
- **Bassari Country** [3] — three cultural areas with terraces, paddy fields and villages, inscribed
  as a living cultural landscape.

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Léopold Sédar Senghor** | poet, statesman, champion of **Négritude**; **the first African elected to the Académie française** [1]; wrote the **national anthem's words** | 1960 (anthem) | French | [1], [2], [4] |
| **Mariama Bâ** (1929–1981) | ***Une si longue lettre*** (*So Long a Letter*) — epistolary novel on **polygamy, marital betrayal, societal expectation and women's resilience** | **1979** | French | [4] |
| **Ousmane Sembène** | film director, producer and writer — **"the father of African film"** | `[NEEDS years]` | French / Wolof | [4] |

**Senghor is the figure this project has been circling all along.** A poet who became a head of state,
led the **Négritude** movement — the argument that African cultural identity was a thing to be
asserted, not apologised for — **wrote his country's national anthem**, was the **first African elected
to the Académie française** [1], and then **retired** [1].

**Négritude is the intellectual ancestor of "Reclaiming African Voices".** The hackathon brief this
project was built for is, in substance, a Négritude proposition. Senghor should be named in the
submission narrative — and so should the critique of him, because Négritude was contested by other
African writers as essentialising `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Presenting Senghor without that argument would
be the flattening this project refuses.**

**Ousmane Sembène matters for a different reason.** Source [4] calls him the father of African film —
and he turned from novels to cinema **because film could reach people who did not read French**
`[NEEDS SOURCE]`. If that holds, it is the most directly relevant fact in this entire folder for an
app that uses **cinematic narration and audio** to carry literature to people the printed page misses.
**Verify it — it may be the best single justification for Ubuntu Heritage's own format.**

**Mariama Bâ's *So Long a Letter* (1979)** [4] sits with Emecheta's *The Joys of Motherhood* (1979,
[`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) — the same year, two West African women writing marriage and
motherhood as political subjects.

## Heritage & sites

**Seven** UNESCO World Heritage Sites [3] — the second-largest count in the folder:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | Shared | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Island of Gorée** | 1978 | Cultural | — | A **"slave-trading centre on the African coast during the Atlantic slave trade period"** |
| Djoudj National Bird Sanctuary | 1981 | Natural | — | Wetland for water birds in the Senegal River delta; **in danger 1984–88 and 2000–06** |
| Niokolo-Koba National Park | 1981 | Natural | — | Gallery forests and large mammals along the Gambia River; **in danger 2007–2024** |
| Island of Saint-Louis | 2000 | Cultural | — | Colonial trading hub; **capital of Senegal 1872–1957** |
| **Stone Circles of Senegambia** | 2006 | Cultural | **The Gambia** | Megalithic stone circles and tumuli, **3rd c. BCE – 16th c. CE** |
| Saloum Delta | 2011 | Cultural | — | Inhabited for millennia; **shell mounds** left by fishing communities |
| Bassari Country | 2012 | Cultural | — | Three cultural areas: terraces, paddy fields, villages and archaeological sites |

- **Niokolo-Koba came off the danger list in 2024** [3] — the most recent recovery in this folder,
  after seventeen years. With Ghadamès ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)), Ichkeul
  ([`tn-tunisia.md`](tn-tunisia.md)), Comoé ([`ci-cote-d-ivoire.md`](ci-cote-d-ivoire.md)) and Abomey
  ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)), that is **five African sites pulled back from danger** — a pattern
  worth stating, because the usual story is only of loss.
- **The Saloum Delta's shell mounds** [3] are heritage made by fishing communities over millennia —
  ordinary people's accumulated work, inscribed. Compare Botswana's Kaditshwene middens
  ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ***Koste Seselwa*** (Seychellois Creole)
- **Title (English):** "Seychellois Unite"
- **Adopted:** **18 June 1996**, on National Day
- **Words and music by:** David François Marc André and George Charles Robert Payet (both, jointly)
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The anthem is titled and sung in the creole** [2] — not in English or French, the two European
> official languages. **Of the eleven creoles above, this is the only one confirmed here to carry a
> national anthem in its own name.**
>
> **Adopted for the transition to multi-party governance** [2], replacing the previous national song —
> **the same reason Mozambique changed its anthem in 2002** ([`mz-mozambique.md`](mz-mozambique.md))
> and the change Angola still has under review ([`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)). **The thirteenth
> politically-changed anthem in this research.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The third uninhabited-before-settlement country in this folder**, after São Tomé and Príncipe
  ([`st-sao-tome-and-principe.md`](st-sao-tome-and-principe.md)) and Mauritius
  ([`mu-mauritius.md`](mu-mauritius.md)). **All three were populated by the slave trade; all three
  made creoles; in all three the creole is what the nation actually speaks.** Written together, these
  files make an argument no single one can: **that African nationhood does not require a precolonial
  past, and that a language made under enslavement is a heritage, not a deficit.**
- **"15 white settlers and enslaved persons" (1770)** [1] — the founding population, stated in one
  clause. **The enslaved are not counted.** That sentence is worth quoting exactly as it stands,
  because the omission is the record.
- ⚠️ **Slavery then indenture, in the same sequence as Mauritius** [1] —
  ([`mu-mauritius.md`](mu-mauritius.md)) — and **the enslaved came via the African-Arab and Indian
  Ocean trades** [1]. **This is the folder's most explicit statement yet of the Indian Ocean slave
  system**, which now touches Mauritius, Mozambique, Comoros, Tanzania and Seychelles and **is
  documented in none of them.** The single largest shared gap in the Eastern African files.
- **1903: split from Mauritius** [1] — the two island files share an administrative history as well
  as a social one.

## Peoples & cultures

**Source [1]: 97% Seychellois Creoles · 3% others.**

- **97% one group, and that group is a creole people** [1] — the highest such share in the folder.
- ⚠️ **"Creole" here is a national identity, not a mixture to be broken down.** As with Mauritius and
  São Tomé, **do not seek ancestry percentages** — that is where this material turns into race
  science, which [STATUS.md](../STATUS.md) already refused once.
- **Moutya and sega** — Seychellois music forms rooted in the enslaved population `[NEEDS SOURCE]`,
  and absent from source [1]. **Moutya is reportedly on UNESCO's intangible heritage list** — verify.
  **If it holds it belongs with Burundi's royal drumming ([`bi-burundi.md`](bi-burundi.md)) and
  Congolese rumba ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)) as living heritage this folder keeps finding
  and never confirms.** **Three unverified intangible-heritage leads is now a pattern worth one
  dedicated research pass.**

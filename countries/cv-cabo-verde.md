## Languages

| Language | Status | Also spoken in SA? | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Portuguese | **Official** | No | [1] |
| Cape Verdean Creole (Kriolu / Crioulo) | **Recognised national language** — "spoken by the vast majority of the population" | No | [1] |

**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** both.

**This is the clearest official-≠-spoken case in the folder.** Source [1] states plainly that
Portuguese is official while **Kriolu is what the vast majority actually speak**. Compare Botswana,
where English is official and Setswana national ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)) — the same
two-tier arrangement, and in Botswana's case the app resolved it by leading with the *national*
language, not the official one. **If `cv` is ever wired, the same logic says lead with Kriolu** — which
the app does not have. The principle is already decided; only the capability is missing.

**Kriolu is a creole, and that is a heritage claim, not a technicality.** It formed among people
brought together by the Atlantic slave trade on these islands. A language born of that crossing is
itself a survival — the kind of thing this project exists to name.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *Cântico da Liberdade* (Portuguese)
- **Title (English):** "Song of Freedom"
- **Adopted:** 5 July 1996
- **Words by:** Amílcar Spencer Lopes
- **Music by:** Adalberto Higino Tavares Silva
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **From 1975 to 1996, Cabo Verde and Guinea-Bissau sang the identical anthem** [2] — a deliberate
> expression of the plan to unify the two countries, which had won independence from Portugal through
> the same liberation movement. **When the merger failed, Cabo Verde wrote itself a new one.**
>
> This is a two-country fact and both files must carry it: see
> [`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md). An anthem is usually presented as timeless; here it is
> the audible record of a political union that did not happen.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **"Prospered from the Atlantic slave trade" is source [1]'s phrase, and the prosperity was the
  trade.** Cabo Verde was an entrepôt: a staging post where people were held and shipped. The
  islands' population, language and music all descend from that fact. **This cannot be a footnote to
  a story about beautiful islands**, and Cidade Velha's World Heritage inscription is inseparable
  from it — see Heritage below.
- **Amílcar Cabral belongs to two countries and to the whole continent.** He founded a single party
  for **Guinea and Cape Verde** [1] and is among the most important theorists of African liberation.
  He is also credited as the lyricist of Guinea-Bissau's anthem — the shared one. Keep
  [`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md) consistent with this file.
- **2024's malaria certification** [1] is a rare present-tense good-news milestone in this folder, and
  worth keeping for exactly that reason.

## Peoples & cultures

- **Ancestry** — source [1]: "The Cape Verdean people trace their ancestry primarily to **West African
  populations**, with additional contributions from early Portuguese settlers and other groups."
  Population approximately **530,000** (2026) [1].
- ⚠️ **No percentage breakdown, and none should be invented.** A creole population is precisely the
  case where ancestry percentages become race science — the risk STATUS.md already refused over the
  admixture table. Source [1]'s sentence is the right level of precision; leave it there.
- **Morna** — a lament, described by source [4] as comparable to the Portuguese *fado*; **coladeras**
  are mornas at a faster tempo. **Cesária Évora** is named as one of Cabo Verde's most popular
  musicians, famous inside the islands and abroad for both [4].
- **Emigration is a national theme, not a footnote.** *Chiquinho* is described as a novel about "the
  step that many Cape Verdeans had to take to achieve a better life: emigration" [4]. A country whose
  foundational novel is about leaving it.

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Baltasar Lopes da Silva | ***Chiquinho*** — coming-of-age novel; the best-known literary work from Cabo Verde | written **1936**, published **1947** | Portuguese | [4] |
| Baltasar Lopes da Silva (as **Osvaldo Alcântara**) | poetry | 1936–60 | Portuguese | [4] |
| Eugénio Tavares | named as a key figure of the period | `[NEEDS SOURCE]` | `[NEEDS SOURCE]` | [4] |

**The *Claridade* movement (1936–1960) is the thing to understand here.** Source [4] describes the
magazine *Claridade* ("Clarity") as the centre of an artistic movement that **marked a break with
Portuguese literary traditions and established a Cabo Verdean identity**, drawing on local themes and
Creole culture.

**The connection worth making.** A magazine that deliberately breaks from the metropole's literature
in order to establish a national one is exactly the move Ubuntu Heritage's four pillars represent for
South Africa — and it happened here in **1936**, in print, with a name and a date. *Claridade* is the
West African evidence that this was a **continental** project, not four South African individuals.
Put it beside Hazoumé's *Doguicimi* (1938, [`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)) and Plaatje's *Mhudi*
(published 1930) and the same decade is doing the same work in three colonies of three different
empires.

## Heritage & sites

**One** UNESCO World Heritage Site [3]:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cidade Velha, Historic Centre of Ribeira Grande** | 2009 | Cultural | "The first European colonial outpost in the tropics", on Santiago; original street layout, two churches, a royal fortress, and a 16th-century marble **pillar at Pillory Square** |

Nine properties are on the tentative list, including **Fogo Natural Park**, the **Tarrafal concentration
camp**, Praia and São Filipe historic centres, and the **Pedra de Lume salt works** [3].

- ⚠️ **The pillory is the point.** A *pelourinho* is where enslaved people were publicly punished.
  Source [3] names it as a feature of the site without saying so. **Any use of Cidade Velha in the app
  must say what that pillar is**, or the app becomes another brochure about a pretty colonial town.
- **The Tarrafal camp on the tentative list** [3] was a Portuguese political prison — 20th-century
  repression, not the 16th. Cabo Verde's heritage list spans both ends of the same empire.

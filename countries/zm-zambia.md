**Three countries, three naming conventions, and possibly one language.** This is exactly the
"same name ≠ same language" trap from [README.md](README.md) — in its harder form, where **different
names may mean one language and the same name may cover different varieties.** **Nothing should be
wired for `zm`, `mw` or the Zimbabwe entry until this is resolved by a linguist-grade source.**
Logged as the file's top open question.

**Tumbuka appears in both Zambia and Malawi** [1] ([`mw-malawi.md`](mw-malawi.md)); **Tonga and Lozi
reach into Zimbabwe and Namibia** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**`zm` is technically wirable** (`lead: "en"`) and would need the Zimbabwe-style honest comment —
**English is official and no more than a third of the country speaks any single indigenous language.**

## 🔴 National anthem — Sontonga again

- **Title (own language):** ***Lumbanyeni Zambia*** (Bemba)
- **Title (English):** "Stand and Sing of Zambia, Proud and Free"
- **Adopted:** 24 October 1964 — independence day
- **Words:** written **collectively**
- **Music by:** **Enoch Mankayi Sontonga**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Source [2]: at independence Zambia took the melody of "God Bless Africa" and wrote its own lyrics
> for it** — and names the melody's other users as **Zimbabwe, Tanzania and South Africa**.
>
> 🔴 **That is four countries in this folder singing one Xhosa schoolteacher's tune.** Tanzania's file
> ([`tz-tanzania.md`](tz-tanzania.md)) records that Tanzania adopted it first, in 1961; Zambia in
> 1964; South Africa's is in the app already. ⚠️ **Source [2] here adds Zimbabwe to the list — which
> [`zw-zimbabwe.md`](zw-zimbabwe.md) must check**, since Zimbabwe is widely understood to have changed
> to a different anthem in 1994 `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Source [2]'s "previously or currently" is doing
> real work in that sentence and should not be read as present tense.**
>
> **This is the strongest single artefact the whole research has produced for Ubuntu Heritage.** A
> South African composition that became the national music of multiple African states, each writing
> its own words to it. **The app is built around four South African literary pillars; Sontonga is the
> fifth figure, and his reach is continental.**
>
> ⚠️ **And source [2] records a contest over the words.** In **2005, women's movement representatives
> asked for the gendered language to be changed; officials declined**, saying the anthem comprised
> **"historical lyrics that reflect the country's heritage."** **That is a live argument about who a
> national text speaks for, and it is exactly the kind of thing this project should present as an
> argument rather than resolve.** Compare the note on "Land of Upright Men" in
> [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md).

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **1888: a chartered company obtained mineral rights from the Lozi leadership** [1]. **That
  sentence is how a large part of Southern Africa was colonised** — not by conquest first but by
  concession, signed by African rulers with a company. **It is the same British South Africa Company
  that operated in Zimbabwe, and the same mechanism the Three Dikgosi went to London in 1895 to
  *escape*** ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)). **Botswana's file tells the successful refusal.
  Zambia's tells what happened where it was not refused.** Written together, those two files make one
  of the strongest arguments in this whole research.
- **The Maravi Empire (1480) reaches both Zambia and Malawi** [1]
  ([`mw-malawi.md`](mw-malawi.md)) — one state, two modern countries, and one of them named after it.
- **1991: a founding president loses an election and leaves** [1] — as in Benin the same year
  ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)). **Two files, one year, the same democratic transition, and neither
  is the story usually told about African politics.**
- **The copper collapse** [1] is the economic hinge, and it is the reason the 1970s and 80s went the
  way they did.

## Literature & voices

The one literary fact this file holds is that **Zambia's anthem lyrics were "written collectively"**
[2] — and that **a request to change them was refused in 2005** [2]. **A national text with no single
author and a live dispute about its words is, in its way, a literature question.**

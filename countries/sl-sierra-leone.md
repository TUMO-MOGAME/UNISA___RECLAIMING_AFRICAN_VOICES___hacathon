**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** Krio, Mende, Temne.

🔴 **Krio is spoken by 97% of the population [1] — and the Krio *people* are 1.3% of it** [1].

Set those two numbers beside each other and something important appears. **A creole that began among
a small settler community of formerly enslaved people has become the language of virtually the entire
country, without being official and without a state imposing it.** Nothing else in this folder
demonstrates so plainly that *what a nation speaks* and *what its law names* are different questions —
the "official ≠ spoken" trap from [README.md](README.md) at its absolute limit.

**And it is the most-spoken language of any country in this research.** Wolof reaches 80% of Senegal
([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)); Kriol reaches 94% of Guinea-Bissau
([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)); **Krio reaches 97%.** All three are creoles born out
of the Atlantic slave trade, and all three outperform the colonial language that outranks them.
**That is a finding about the whole region, not one country**, and it belongs in the submission
narrative.

**`sl` is technically wirable** (`lead: "en"`) and would be **more misleading here than anywhere else
in the folder** — English is official and 97% of people speak something else. If it is added at all,
the Zimbabwe-style honest comment is mandatory, with Krio named first in `notYet`.

## National anthem

- **Title:** "High We Exalt Thee, Realm of the Free" (English)
- **Adopted:** 27 April 1961 — independence day itself
- **Words by:** **Clifford Nelson Fyle** — an academic at **Fourah Bay College**
- **Music by:** **John Joseph Akar** — the first post-independence Director of the **Sierra Leone
  Broadcasting Service**, who also **founded the national dance troupe**
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Both authors were cultural institution-builders, not officials** [2]: a college academic and the
> man who ran the national broadcaster and started the national dance company. Compare Senegal, where
> the poet-president wrote the words ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)), and Guinea-Bissau, where the
> liberation theorist did ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)). **In West Africa the people
> who wrote the anthems were frequently the people building the culture**, which is a pattern worth
> naming.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **Freetown and Liberia are the same experiment, run twice.** Sierra Leone was settled from 1787
  by formerly enslaved people — the **Nova Scotian Settlers** [1] — under British sponsorship;
  Liberia from 1822 by formerly enslaved people under American sponsorship
  ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)). **Both established settler communities among peoples already
  living there**, and in both the descendants of the settlers became a distinct social class. **The
  two files must be written consistently**, and neither can be told as a simple homecoming.
- **Krio at 1.3% of the population but 97% of its speech** [1] is the long consequence of that
  settlement — the settlers' language became everyone's while the settlers stayed a tiny minority.
  A rare case where a colonised-then-creolised language *won* without power behind it.
- 🔴 **"Domestic slavery abolished in 1928"** [1]. Alongside **The Gambia's 1906**
  ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)) and **Mauritania's 1905 / 1981 / 2007**
  ([`mr-mauritania.md`](mr-mauritania.md)), this folder now documents **three West African countries
  where slavery persisted legally into the twentieth century** — in a British colony founded as a
  settlement for freed slaves. That contradiction is the most important thing in this file.
- **Bai Bureh (1898)** [1] joins Samori Touré ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)), Omar Mukhtar
  ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)) and Kgosi Sechele ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)) in this
  folder's record of armed anti-colonial resistance. **A hut tax as the trigger** is worth keeping:
  the war began over taxation of people's own homes.
- **Fourah Bay College, 1827** [1] — widely described as the oldest Western-style university in West
  Africa `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. If it holds, it matters: a degree-granting institution in West Africa
  operating for nearly two centuries, and the lyricist of the national anthem taught there [2].

- **Two groups at almost exactly a third each** — Mende and Temne [1] — with no majority. Sierra
  Leone's politics has run along that line `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.
- **Mende, Susu, Fula, Mandingo and Kono all cross into Liberia and Guinea**
  ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md), [`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)). The **Mende** also have their
  own script — the **Kikakui syllabary** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — which if confirmed adds a third indigenous
  West African writing system to Vai ([`lr-liberia.md`](lr-liberia.md)) and N'Ko
  ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)), and supports the "at least 27 scripts" claim recorded in the
  Liberia file. **Worth chasing.**

# Congo (Republic)

**ISO code:** `cg` · **Region:** Middle (Central) Africa (UN M49)
**Researched by:** Tumo Olorato Mogame, with Claude · **Last updated:** 2026-08-30
**Status:** core research — languages, anthem, milestones and peoples sourced · literature empty · heritage partial

> ⚠️ **Two different countries are called Congo.** This file is the **Republic of the Congo**
> (capital **Brazzaville**, ISO `cg`). Its neighbour is the **Democratic Republic of the Congo**
> (capital **Kinshasa**, ISO `cd`) — see [`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md). They are separate
> states with different colonial histories, different official language arrangements and different
> anthems. Confusing them is the single easiest error to make in this folder.

> **How this file is put together.** Built **claim by claim**: every row names the source it came
> from, and the numbered list at the bottom is what those names point at. Sources are
> encyclopaedia-grade — rough sources are fine at this stage ([README.md](README.md)), assertions made
> to a reader are not.

**Both national languages are contact languages, and both cross the river.** Kituba and Lingala [1]
are also national languages of the DRC ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)) — **the same languages,
in two countries, split by a river and by which European empire arrived**. That is the clearest
same-language-two-states case in the folder after Hausa in Niger and Nigeria
([`ne-niger.md`](ne-niger.md), [`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *La Congolaise* (French)
- **Title (English):** "The Congolese"
- **Adopted:** **4 November 1959** · replaced **31 December 1969** · **restored 10 June 1991**
- **Words by:** unknown [2]
- **Music by:** unknown [2]
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Dropped for the People's Republic, brought back when it ended** [2] — the anthem was replaced on
> **31 December 1969**, the same day the **People's Republic of the Congo was proclaimed** [1], and
> restored in 1991, months before that state was formally disestablished in March 1992 [1]. **The
> anthem and the regime rise and fall together, to the day.**
>
> Source [2] adds that the restoration distinguished the country from its eastern neighbour — the
> anthem is part of how these two Congos tell themselves apart.
>
> **That is now the ninth politically altered anthem in this research** (with Togo, Ghana, Libya,
> Tunisia, Nigeria, Niger, Mauritania and Angola's pending review). The pattern is no longer
> anecdotal.

**Framing notes.**

- **The Kingdom of Loango (c. 1550–1883)** [1] is a major African state that ran for over three
  centuries and is almost unknown outside the region. With the **Kingdom of Kongo**
  ([`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)), it means the Atlantic coast of Central Africa had **large,
  durable, documented African states before and throughout the period of European contact**. Neither
  has a proper entry anywhere in this folder yet.
- **1484 appears in both this file and Angola's** [1] — one Portuguese voyage, two modern countries.
  The whole coast from Gabon to Angola was a single political and commercial world before it was cut
  into five colonies.
- ⚠️ **Loango's dates end in 1883, and the reason is the slave trade and then colonisation**
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. Source [1] gives the bracket without the cause. That gap needs filling before the
  kingdom is presented as simply having "ended".

- **The Bakongo are 41% here** [1] and are also the third-largest group in Angola (14%,
  [`ao-angola.md`](ao-angola.md)), as well as a major people of the DRC. **One people, three
  countries** — the descendants of the Kingdom of Kongo, now divided by three colonial borders and two
  European languages.
- ⚠️ **"Mbenga Pygmies" is source [1]'s wording.** As in [`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md), the
  exonym should be replaced with the peoples' own names. **Note that source [1] does at least give
  "Mbenga"**, which is a step better than the bare exonym — use that.
- **The Bateke** give their name to the Teke Plateau `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

# Côte d'Ivoire

**ISO code:** `ci` · **Region:** Western Africa (UN M49)
**Researched by:** Tumo Olorato Mogame, with Claude · **Last updated:** 2026-08-30
**Status:** core research — languages, anthem, milestones, peoples, literature and heritage sourced

> ⚠️ **Use the name "Côte d'Ivoire", not "Ivory Coast".** Source [1] records that **in April 1986 the
> government declared Côte d'Ivoire to be its formal name for diplomatic purposes and has since
> officially refused to recognise any translation of it** in its international dealings. Translating
> the name is not a neutral convenience — the country has explicitly asked that it not be done. The
> app's country list should follow this, and it already does
> ([`anthems.ts`](../app/src/content/anthems.ts) uses "Côte d'Ivoire").

> **How this file is put together.** Built **claim by claim**: every row names the source it came
> from, and the numbered list at the bottom is what those names point at. Sources are
> encyclopaedia-grade — rough sources are fine at this stage ([README.md](README.md)), assertions made
> to a reader are not.

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *L'Abidjanaise* (French)
- **Title (English):** "Song of Abidjan"
- **Adopted:** 7 August 1960 — independence day itself
- **Words by:** Mathieu Ekra, Joachim Bony and Pierre Marie Coty
- **Music by:** Pierre Marie Coty and Pierre Michel Pango
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **An anthem named after a city, not a country** [2] — Abidjan, the largest city and the capital at
> independence. Source [2] describes it as celebrating national dignity, unity and fraternity. Note
> that the capital has since moved to Yamoussoukro `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, which would leave the anthem
> named for a city that is no longer the seat of state — worth confirming before it is used.

**Framing notes.**

- **The independence movement started in a farmers' union** [1]. That is a different story from the
  one usually told about African independence — not only lawyers and soldiers, but cocoa growers
  organising against forced labour and price-fixing. It also explains 1979: the cocoa economy is the
  thread running from 1944 to the civil wars.
- **Grand-Bassam is inscribed as a *colonial* capital** [3] — see the note under Heritage. A World
  Heritage listing for the architecture of one's own colonisation is a genuinely difficult category,
  and this file should not pretend otherwise.
- **The 2021 census counts 22% of the population as non-Ivorian** [1]. That is a very large migrant
  share and it is directly connected to the cocoa economy and to the citizenship politics behind the
  civil wars. Handle with care and with a better source than this one.

*(The sub-shares are of the Ivorian population, not of the whole.)*

- ⚠️ **"Ivorian" vs "non-Ivorian" is a political category, not an ethnic one.** The concept of
  *ivoirité* — who counts as authentically Ivorian — was central to the conflict of 1999–2007.
  Reproducing this table without saying so would hand a reader a contested political frame as neutral
  demography. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` for the *ivoirité* claim; do not use the table until it is in hand.
- **Gur / Voltaic and Senoufo peoples** appear here and in Burkina Faso
  ([`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md)); **Mande** peoples connect to Mali and Guinea. These are
  regional, not national, groupings.

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ahmadou Kourouma (1927–2003) | ***Les Soleils des indépendances*** (*The Suns of Independence*) — satire of contemporary African politics | **1968** | French | [4] |
| Ahmadou Kourouma | *En attendant le vote des bêtes sauvages* (*Waiting for the Wild Beasts to Vote*) | 1998 | French | [4] |
| Ahmadou Kourouma | *Allah n'est pas obligé* (*Allah is Not Obliged*) | 2000 | French | [4] |
| Bernard Binlin Dadié | *contes*, plays and poems | `[NEEDS years]` | French | [4] |
| Véronique Tadjo | *L'Ombre d'Imana* — on the Rwandan genocide | `[NEEDS year]` | French | [4] |

**Kourouma is the most directly relevant writer in the West African files so far.** Source [4]:
he **"revolutionized the French language by using it in an unconventional and innovative way"**, and
*Les Soleils des indépendances* "has been read by countless students all over Africa and translated
into numerous foreign languages".

**Why that matters here.** Kourouma wrote French as a Malinké speaker thinks — bending the coloniser's
grammar to carry an African oral idiom rather than submitting to it. That is *the* central technical
problem of this entire project: how do you carry an oral inheritance into a written form without
letting the form flatten it? Vilakazi solved it by writing isiZulu poetry in received European metre
and then breaking it; Kourouma solved it by breaking French. **These two belong in the same sentence.**

- **Bernard Dadié** is described as **the first Ivorian writer to gain international recognition, while
  the country was still a colony** [4], working to valorise African traditions against modernity — the
  same generation and the same project as Hazoumé in Benin and the *Claridade* group in Cabo Verde.
- **Véronique Tadjo's *L'Ombre d'Imana*** is called one of the most powerful texts written by an
  African author on the Rwandan genocide [4]. Cross-reference [`rw-rwanda.md`](rw-rwanda.md) when that
  file is written — a genocide narrated by a writer from another African country is a deliberate act
  of continental solidarity, not tourism.

## Heritage & sites

Five UNESCO World Heritage Sites [3] — **four of them natural**, an unusual profile:

| Site | Inscribed | Type | Shared | In danger | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mount Nimba Strict Nature Reserve | 1982 | Natural | **Guinea** | **Yes** | A "sky island" of local microclimates with rich endemic flora and fauna |
| Taï National Park | 1982 | Natural | — | No | Primary tropical rainforest; **150+ endemic plant species** |
| Comoé National Park | 1983 | Natural | — | No — **danger-listed 2003–2017, then removed** | Rainforest, savanna and gallery forest along the Komoé; **500 bird species** |
| Historic Town of Grand-Bassam | 2012 | Cultural | — | No | The 1880s French colonial capital; colonial architecture adapted to the tropics |
| **Sudanese-style mosques of northern Côte d'Ivoire** | 2021 | Cultural | — | No | **Eight mudbrick mosques, 17th–19th centuries**, Sudano-Sahelian style |

- **The mosques are the entry point, not Grand-Bassam.** Eight mudbrick mosques built between the 17th
  and 19th centuries [3] are precolonial African architecture, still standing, still in use — the
  same Sudano-Sahelian building tradition that runs to Mali's Djenné. Compare the earthen architecture
  at Ait-Ben-Haddou ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)) and Ghadamès ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)):
  a single trans-Saharan building culture with sites in at least four of these files.
- **Comoé came off the danger list in 2017** [3] — the fourth recovery in this folder.

Swahili is **not** stated as official by source [1].

**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** Kirundi and French.

🔴 **Kirundi is both official *and* the sole national language** [1] — a stronger position than almost
any indigenous language in this folder. It is not one of several national languages beside a dominant
European one (Senegal's six, [`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md); Ghana's ten,
[`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)); **it stands alone as the national language while French and English are
merely official.**

**Burundi is also one of the few countries here with a single dominant indigenous language** rather
than dozens — which makes it, unusually, a country the app's model would actually fit. **If Ubuntu
Heritage ever adds a twelfth language, Kirundi is a simpler target than Swahili** — though Swahili
reaches far more people (see [`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)).

**`bi` is technically wirable** (`lead: "en"`) and would be **badly misleading** — English is official
and Kirundi is what the country speaks. If added, the Zimbabwe-style honest comment is mandatory, with
Kirundi first in `notYet`.

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ***Burundi Bwâcu*** (Kirundi)
- **Title (English):** "Our Beloved Burundi"
- **Adopted:** June 1962
- **Words by:** **Jean-Baptiste Ntahokaja**, a Catholic priest, **leading a group of writers**
- **Music by:** Marc Barengayabo
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **The title is in Kirundi, not French** [2] — which in this folder is rare. Most anthems here carry
> a French or Portuguese title even when an indigenous version exists; Burundi's is known by its own
> language's name. Compare the CAR, where *La Renaissance* leads and *E Zingo* follows
> ([`cf-central-african-republic.md`](cf-central-african-republic.md)).
>
> **Lyrics exist in both Kirundi and French** [2] — the third bilingual anthem in this research, after
> Cameroon's French/English ([`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md)) and the CAR's French/Sango. **If the
> app ever uses this anthem, the Kirundi version is the one that serves the project's purpose.**
>
> **Written by a group of writers rather than an individual** [2] — like Chad's teacher-and-students
> ([`td-chad.md`](td-chad.md)) and Ghana's public contest ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)).

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **Read the Hutu/Tutsi categories with the colonial record in front of you.** Belgian and German
  administration hardened what had been fluid social categories into fixed, documented ethnic
  identities — issuing identity cards and ruling through one group `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **This is
  essential context and it is not in source [1].** Without it, the 1972 and 1993 events read as
  ancient tribal hatred, which is precisely the colonial-era explanation this project exists to
  refuse. **Ubuntu Heritage must not touch this subject until that context is properly sourced.**
- ⚠️ **The death figures are wide ranges from a single encyclopaedia source** [1]. Do not narrow them,
  do not pick a number, and do not use either figure as atmosphere.
- **Burundi and Rwanda were administered together as Ruanda-Urundi** [1] — see
  [`rw-rwanda.md`](rw-rwanda.md). **The two files must be written consistently**, and neither country's
  history should be told as a footnote to the other's.
- **A four-century monarchy abolished in 1966** [1]. The Kingdom of Burundi ran from the late
  sixteenth century — comparable in length to Morocco's Alawi dynasty
  ([`ma-morocco.md`](ma-morocco.md)) and Kanem–Bornu ([`td-chad.md`](td-chad.md)). **Source [1] gives
  it one line.**

## Peoples & cultures

Source [1] gives two sets of figures and they disagree — **recorded here as the source records them,
unreconciled**:

| Group | Share | Alternative figure |
|---|---|---|
| Hutu | 85% | ~81% |
| Tutsi | 14% | ~18% |
| **Twa** | 1% | fewer than 1% |

- 🔴 **See the framing note above before using this table at all.** These categories have a colonial
  administrative history and presenting them as neutral demography would repeat it.
- **The Twa** are one of the forest peoples referred to elsewhere in this folder by the exonym "Pygmy"
  ([`cm-cameroon.md`](cm-cameroon.md), [`cg-congo-republic.md`](cg-congo-republic.md)). **Source [1]
  uses their own name, which is the right practice** — and at under 1% they are the most marginalised
  group in the country. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` on their situation; it should not be left at a percentage.
- ⚠️ **Burundi's royal drumming (*ingoma*)** is, by widespread account, inscribed on UNESCO's
  intangible cultural heritage list `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — **verify, because if it holds it is one of the
  strongest living-heritage entries available to this project**: a court performance tradition from
  the kingdom abolished in 1966, still practised. **Do not assert it without a source.**

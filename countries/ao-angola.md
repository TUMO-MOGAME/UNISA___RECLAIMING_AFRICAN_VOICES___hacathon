**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all seven.

🔴 **Oshiwambo is the connection this folder has been waiting for.** It is a recognised national
language of Angola [1] — **and it is already named in the app**, in
[`country-languages.ts`](../app/src/content/country-languages.ts), under Namibia's `notYet` list.
Ubuntu Heritage therefore *already knows this language exists and does not have it*, for a different
country.

That makes Angola the first country outside Southern Africa where the app's existing data and this
research touch. ⚠️ **But apply the README's first trap before acting on it**: "same name ≠ same
language". Angola's Oshiwambo and Namibia's Oshiwambo need to be confirmed as **the same language**
across the border before anything links them — that is exactly the check that stopped Zimbabwe's
Ndebele being mapped to South Africa's isiNdebele.

**Angola is Bantu-speaking, and that matters here.** Umbundu, Kimbundu, Kikongo and Oshiwambo are
Bantu languages, related — distantly — to Setswana, isiZulu and the rest of the app's eleven
`[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Every West and North African file so far has recorded language families with no
relation to the app's languages at all. Angola is where that changes**, and the Journey's story of
Bantu migration (already in the app's Atlas material) reaches this coast.

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *Angola Avante* (Portuguese)
- **Title (English):** "Forward Angola"
- **Adopted:** 1975
- **Words by:** Manuel Rui Alves Monteiro
- **Music by:** Rui Alberto Vieira Dias Minga
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> ⚠️ **The anthem is officially under review.** Source [2]: a **commission has been appointed to study
> possible changes**, because the lyrics reference "important dates and events for the previous sole
> ruling party" which became obsolete when multi-party elections were introduced.
>
> **An anthem that is a one-party document in a multi-party state** is the eighth case in this research
> of a national song being politically contested — after Togo, Ghana, Libya, Tunisia, Nigeria, Niger
> and Mauritania. **This is now a well-evidenced pattern rather than a run of coincidences**, and it
> should shape how `/countries` presents anthems.
>
> **The lyricist, Manuel Rui Monteiro, is a novelist** `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — which if confirmed adds
> Angola to Senegal ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)) and Guinea-Bissau
> ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)) as countries whose anthem words were written by a
> literary figure.

**Framing notes.**

- ⚠️ **Angola was at war for forty-one of the fifty years between 1961 and 2002** [1] — a liberation
  war straight into a civil war with no gap. **"Independence achieved; civil war began immediately"**
  is source [1]'s own sequence, and it is the central fact of modern Angola. Any app treatment must
  carry it; independence here was not an ending.
- ⚠️ **The Kingdom of Kongo (from 1484) is the story this file most needs and least has.** It was a
  major African state that exchanged ambassadors with Portugal and the Vatican `[NEEDS SOURCE]` — and
  it was progressively destroyed by the slave trade that Portugal ran through it. **It also spans
  Angola, both Congos and Gabon**, so it belongs across four files, like the Mali Empire in West
  Africa. Currently one line in this file.
- **Berlin, 1884–85** [1] appears here explicitly. Angola is a good place to state the general rule —
  the borders of most files in this folder were drawn at that conference — rather than repeating it
  fifty times.
- **The 1836 abolition** [1] is early compared with The Gambia's 1906
  ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)) and Sierra Leone's 1928 ([`sl-sierra-leone.md`](sl-sierra-leone.md)).
  ⚠️ Note carefully that source [1] says the **trade** was abolished — that is not the same as slavery
  ending, and the difference has been used to make abolition look earlier than it was.

## Peoples & cultures

**2024 census** [1] — the most recent demographic data in the folder:

| Group | Share |
|---|---|
| **Ovimbundu** | 29% |
| **Ambundu** | 27% |
| **Bakongo** | 14% |
| Others | **30%** |

- **"Others" at 30%** [1] is the largest unnamed share in any table here, and it hides the Chokwe,
  Luchazi and Ovambo peoples whose languages source [1] recognises nationally. **Naming languages but
  not peoples is an odd asymmetry** — the language list is the better guide.
- **The Bakongo are the people of the Kingdom of Kongo**, and they live across Angola, both Congos and
  Gabon `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. The name of two modern countries comes from their kingdom.
- **Ovambo / Oshiwambo speakers cross into Namibia**, where they are the largest group
  ([`na-namibia.md`](na-namibia.md) — already in the app's data). **This is the first documented
  people in the folder who straddle the border into the app's existing Southern African coverage.**

## Literature & voices

| Writer | Work | Year | Language | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Agostinho Neto** | poetry — **"Angola's preeminent poet"**; led the MPLA through the war and was **first president, 1975–1979** | — | Portuguese | [4] |
| **Pepetela** | ***Mayombe*** — on the ethnic, ideological and sexual tensions inside a group of MPLA guerrillas | **1980** | Portuguese | [4] |
| Pepetela | *A Geração da Utopia* (*The Utopian Generation*) — four revolutionaries from Lisbon student days to disillusionment by the early 1990s | **1992** | Portuguese | [4] |
| **José Eduardo Agualusa** (b. 1960) | magical-realist fiction; **2017 International Dublin Literary Award** | 2017 (prize) | Portuguese | [4] |
| Manuel Rui Alves Monteiro | the national anthem's words | 1975 | Portuguese | [2] |

🔴 **The single most useful fact in this file, for this project:** source [4] records that ***Mayombe*
was so controversial that President Neto himself had to approve its publication in Angola.**

**A poet-president personally deciding whether a novel critical of his own movement could be
published.** That is the relationship between literature and state power, in one sentence — and it
cuts both ways: Neto let it out. Set beside Senghor, the poet-president who wrote Senegal's anthem and
retired voluntarily ([`sn-senegal.md`](sn-senegal.md)), and this folder now documents **two African
states led by poets**, with very different consequences.

**For Ubuntu Heritage's argument** — that literature is not decoration but national infrastructure —
Angola is the strongest evidence and the strongest warning. *Mayombe* (1980) is a liberation-movement
novel that refused to flatter the liberation movement, published anyway. **That is the standard this
project set itself in [AGENTS.md §4](../AGENTS.md): tell it even when it complicates the story.**

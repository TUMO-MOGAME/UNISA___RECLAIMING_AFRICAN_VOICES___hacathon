**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** Swahili and every indigenous Kenyan language.

🔴 **Swahili is both official and national here** [1] — a stronger position than in the DRC, where it
is one of four national languages ([`cd-congo-drc.md`](cd-congo-drc.md)). **This is the second file to
make the case that Swahili should be Ubuntu Heritage's twelfth language**, and it makes it harder to
ignore: Swahili is official in Kenya, national in the DRC, and — on the evidence still to be gathered
— official in Tanzania and Uganda too.

**`ke` is technically wirable** (`lead: "en"`), and doing so would be **precisely the Zimbabwe
mistake**, because the honest lead is a language the app does not have. **Botswana's precedent says
lead with the national language, not the official one** ([`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md)) — and
here they are the same language, Swahili, which the app lacks. **Leave `ke` unmapped until Swahili
exists in the registry**, and record that as the reason.

## 🔴 National anthem — built from a lullaby

- **Title (own language):** ***Ee Mungu Nguvu Yetu*** (Swahili)
- **Title (English):** "Oh God of All Creation"
- **Adopted:** 12 December 1963 — independence day itself
- **Words by:** Graham Hyslop, Thomas Kalume, Peter Kibukosya, Washington Omondi, George W.
  Senoga-Zake
- **Music:** **a traditional melody**, adapted by the same group
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> 🔴 **Source [2]: the melody comes from "a folk song mothers traditionally sang to children."**
>
> **Kenya's national anthem is a lullaby.** Of every anthem in this research — the prison-written
> poem in Algeria ([`dz-algeria.md`](dz-algeria.md)), the Mandinka melody under English words in The
> Gambia ([`gm-gambia.md`](gm-gambia.md)), the Chinese composer's gift to Guinea-Bissau
> ([`gw-guinea-bissau.md`](gw-guinea-bissau.md)) — **this is the one most directly usable by a project
> about carrying inherited voices to the next generation.** A country chose, as its statement of
> itself, the song its mothers already sang.
>
> Source [2] also records that Kenya's is **"the first anthem in Africa to be written by a group
> commissioned by the government"**, and that the commission's brief was explicitly to **preserve the
> original character of the melody** while making it work for both English and Swahili lyrics and for
> military band performance.
>
> **That brief is a design specification for exactly the problem Ubuntu Heritage has**: carry a
> traditional form into an official modern context without flattening it. **Kenya wrote it down in
> 1963.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The Swahili coast from the 1st century CE** [1] and **Zheng He's fleet in 1414** [1] describe an
  Indian Ocean world in which East Africa was a **trading partner of Arabia, India and China centuries
  before Europe arrived**. **This is the single most useful corrective in the Eastern African files**,
  and it belongs beside the trans-Saharan trade documented across the West African ones
  ([`ml-mali.md`](ml-mali.md), [`mr-mauritania.md`](mr-mauritania.md)). **Two ancient African trade
  systems, neither of which involved Europe.**
- ⚠️ **The Mau Mau casualty figures in source [1] are lopsided and should not stand alone.** 11,000
  fighters against 100 British troops [1] is a ratio that describes the nature of the counter-
  insurgency. **The detention-camp system and the far larger civilian toll are not in source [1] at
  all** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`, and the British government's later acknowledgement and settlement is
  absent too. **Publishing source [1]'s numbers without that context would understate what happened.**
  This is one of the more serious `[NEEDS SOURCE]` items in the folder.
- **The Nandi Resistance lasted sixteen years** [1] — longer than Samori Touré's in Guinea
  ([`gn-guinea.md`](gn-guinea.md)) — and is almost unknown outside Kenya.

### 🔴 Ngũgĩ wa Thiong'o is this project's argument, made by a novelist, in 1986

Source [4]: *Decolonising the Mind* is a collection of essays on language and its role in national
culture, history and identity, **in which Ngũgĩ "explained in detail his decision to write in Gikuyu
instead of English."**

**A writer who had already succeeded in English gave it up**, and wrote a book explaining that the
language you write in is not a neutral vehicle. **That is the thesis Ubuntu Heritage is built on** —
that content in English with a language toggle bolted on is not the same as content authored in an
African language, which is why [`languages.ts`](../app/src/i18n/languages.ts) carries a
`reviewedContent` flag and refuses to pass machine text off as authoritative.

**Ngũgĩ should be named in the submission narrative.** He is the strongest external authority for the
choice this project has already made.

⚠️ **And note who source [1] names.** Kenya's encyclopaedia entry lists **one** literary work: *Out of
Africa*, **a Danish settler's memoir of colonial Kenya** [1]. Not Ngũgĩ. Not Grace Ogot. **The
country's most internationally famous "Kenyan literature" is a book by a colonist about her farm.**
That is precisely the erasure this project exists to name, and it happened in the source this file was
built from — **which is itself the best evidence for why the research in this folder needs doing.**

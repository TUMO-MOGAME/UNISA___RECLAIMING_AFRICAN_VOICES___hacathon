## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** ***Rwanda nziza*** (Kinyarwanda)
- **Title (English):** "Rwanda, Our Beautiful Country"
- **Adopted:** **31 December 2001**
- **Words by:** Faustin Murigo
- **Music by:** Jean-Bosco Hashakaimana
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> 🔴 **Rwanda changed its flag and its anthem on the last day of 2001**, and source [2] gives the
> reason plainly: **"a break from their violent and bloody past."** Source [1] adds what changed —
> the new symbols **removed overt ethnic distinctions from official state identity** [1].
>
> **This is the most deliberate act of national-symbol design in the entire folder.** Other countries
> here changed anthems after coups (Ghana, Togo, the Congos), for political systems (Mozambique,
> pending in Angola), or because a coloniser wrote them (Niger). **Rwanda redesigned its symbols to
> stop them from carrying an ethnic division that had been used to organise a genocide.**
>
> Source [2] notes the new anthem "incorporates African musical styles". **The twelfth
> politically-changed anthem in this research — and the one with the clearest stated purpose.**

**Framing notes.**

- 🔴 **The colonial construction of Hutu and Tutsi as fixed categories is the missing context, and it
  is not in source [1].** German and Belgian administration hardened fluid social categories into
  documented ethnic identities and ruled through one of them `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **Without that, the
  1994 events read as ancient tribal hatred — which is the colonial-era explanation this project
  exists to refuse.** The identical warning is in [`bi-burundi.md`](bi-burundi.md); **Rwanda and
  Burundi were administered together as Ruanda-Urundi** [1] and **the research should be done once and
  applied to both.**
- 🔴 **The *gacaca* courts are the item in this file most directly relevant to Ubuntu Heritage.**
  Source [1] calls them **"traditional courts"** used to try genocide cases between 2004 and 2012.
  **An indigenous justice institution, revived and used at national scale for the gravest crimes
  imaginable.**
  **That is the same category of thing as the *kgotla*** — the Tswana assembly where a Kgosi could be
  questioned, already documented in [`bw-botswana.md`](bw-botswana.md) — **and as *Ityala Lamawele*,
  Mqhayi's novel about a customary-law court case, which is one of Ubuntu Heritage's four pillars.**
  ⚠️ **Gacaca is also seriously contested** — its fairness, its due-process standards and its outcomes
  are debated `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. **That contest is part of the story, not a reason to leave it out.**
  **This is the strongest and most difficult connection this research has found to the app's existing
  literary canon.**
- ⚠️ **Source [1] gives no death toll**, and this file deliberately adds none. Figures for 1994 are
  documented in scholarship; **they belong in a sourced document, not in a research note assembled
  from an encyclopaedia.**
- **A kingdom from the 15th century abolished by referendum in 1961** [1] — five centuries, one line,
  as with Burundi's.

## Peoples & cultures

Source [1] describes **three subgroups within the Banyarwanda cultural group**, with **1994** figures:
**Hutu 85% · Tutsi 14% · Twa 1%**.

🔴 **Read the framing note above before using this table, and note what source [1] says next:** the
constitution **"prohibits political organizations from basing themselves on race, ethnic group, tribe,
clan, region, sex, religion or any other division"** [1].

**Rwanda's own state does not classify its citizens this way any more.** ⚠️ **An app that displays a
Hutu/Tutsi/Twa breakdown for Rwanda would be reproducing exactly the classification the country
abolished — using 1994 figures, from the year of the genocide.** **Recommendation: do not publish this
table at all.** It is recorded here because the research rule is to record what the source says; it is
not fit to show a reader.

- **"Banyarwanda" is the term source [1] uses for the cultural group as a whole** — one people, one
  language. **That is the frame the country itself uses**, and it is the one to follow.
- **The Twa** appear again at ~1% [1], as in Burundi ([`bi-burundi.md`](bi-burundi.md)).
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]` on their present situation.

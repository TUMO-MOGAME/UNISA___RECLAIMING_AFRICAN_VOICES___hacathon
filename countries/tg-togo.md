**Official-language instrument:** `[NEEDS SOURCE]`.

**Languages Ubuntu Heritage does not have:** all of them.

**Two national languages, and they map onto the country's politics.** Ewe is the language of the
southern Aja–Ewe–Mina bloc (42.4%) and Kabiye of the Kabye–Tem north (25.9%) [1]. Naming exactly two
national languages in a country with that north–south division is a political settlement expressed as
language policy, not a neutral list. `[NEEDS SOURCE]` before writing it up — but do not present the
pair as arbitrary.

**Ewe crosses into Ghana**, where it is one of the ten government-sponsored languages
([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)) — and the Ghana file records Kofi Awoonor drawing on **Ewe oral
poetry**. **The Ewe were split between British and French Togoland in 1916** [1]. One people, two
colonial partitions, two modern states.

**Same structural blocker as Burkina Faso** — see [`bf-burkina-faso.md`](bf-burkina-faso.md#languages).

## National anthem

- **Title (own language):** *Salut à toi, pays de nos aïeux* (French)
- **Title (English):** "Hail to Thee, Land of Our Forefathers"
- **Adopted:** **1960** · replaced **November 1979** · **restored April 1991** · confirmed by
  constitution **14 October 1992**
- **Words and music by:** **Alex Casimir-Dosseh** (both)
- **Recording we could use:** none sourced yet
- **Source:** [2]

> **Restored when one-party rule ended.** Source [2]: the anthem was replaced in 1979 and brought back
> in 1991 "when Western-mandated reforms were brought in and one-party rule dropped", then written
> into the constitution in 1992.
>
> **That is now five anthems in this folder altered by politics** — Togo, Ghana
> ([`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)), Libya ([`ly-libya.md`](ly-libya.md)), Tunisia
> ([`tn-tunisia.md`](tn-tunisia.md)) and Nigeria ([`ng-nigeria.md`](ng-nigeria.md)) — plus Niger's
> outright replacement ([`ne-niger.md`](ne-niger.md)) and Mauritania's referendum
> ([`mr-mauritania.md`](mr-mauritania.md)). **Seven of the twenty countries researched so far have
> changed their national song within living memory.** Any app feature presenting an anthem as a
> timeless inheritance is misinforming the reader, and this is now well enough evidenced to state
> as a finding rather than a suspicion.

**Framing notes.**

- **Togo was colonised three times over by three different European powers** — German protectorate
  1884, then partitioned between Britain and France in 1916 [1]. Few countries illustrate the
  arbitrariness of African borders more directly: **the same people were reassigned between empires
  by the outcome of a European war they had no part in.**
- **The two coup dates are both 13 January, four years apart** [1]. Source [1] does not comment on it;
  recorded here as what the source says, not as a claim about intent.
- **A presidency passing from father to son in 2005** [1], after a rule that began with the 1967 coup.
  State this plainly; it is the shape of the country's modern politics.
- **Olympio's assassination in 1963 is often described as the first coup in post-independence Africa**
  `[NEEDS SOURCE]`. If it holds it is a significant continental milestone and belongs in more than
  this file.

## Peoples & cultures

**Source [1]** — 94.4% African, 5.6% non-African:

| Group | Share |
|---|---|
| **Aja, Ewe and Mina** | **42.4%** |
| **Kabye and Tem** | **25.9%** |
| Akan and Gurma | 17.1% |
| Kposo and Akebu | 4.1% |
| Ana-Ife | 3.2% |
| Other West African groups | 1.7% |

- **The Batammariba and their *takienta*** — mudbrick tower houses, inscribed as **Koutammakou** and
  **shared with Benin** [3]. See [`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md), which carries the same site from the
  other side of the border. **A cultural landscape split by a line, listed by both countries.**
- **Aja, Ewe, Mina, Gurma and Akan all appear in Benin's and Ghana's tables**
  ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md), [`gh-ghana.md`](gh-ghana.md)). The Gulf of Guinea coast is one
  cultural continuum with four or five states laid across it.
- ⚠️ **Vodun.** Benin's census records Vodun at 11.6% ([`bj-benin.md`](bj-benin.md)) among the same
  Aja-Ewe peoples who make up Togo's largest bloc. Source [1] gives **no religious breakdown for
  Togo** — a gap worth closing, and one that must be handled as a living faith rather than folklore,
  exactly as in the Benin file.

## Heritage & sites

| Site | Inscribed | Type | Shared | What it is |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Koutammakou, the Land of the Batammariba** | **2023** | Cultural | **Benin** | A cultural landscape shaped by the Batammariba, featuring mudbrick tower houses called ***takienta*** | [3] |

⚠️ **Source [1] does not confirm UNESCO status for Koutammakou** — it lists it among Togo's parks and
reserves without saying so. **The inscription details above come from source [3], Benin's World
Heritage list**, which records Koutammakou as shared with Togo. **Togo's own World Heritage list was
not fetched**, so this file may be incomplete.

- **The *takienta* are the third distinct earthen-architecture tradition in this folder**, alongside
  the Sudano-Sahelian mosques and mud cities of Mali, Niger, Côte d'Ivoire, Mauritania and Morocco.
  Different form, same material, same absence from most accounts of world architecture.
